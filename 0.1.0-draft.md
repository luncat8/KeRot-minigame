Important: smooth, realistic physics.

The player throws a puck (like in curling), choosing its direction and speed (a vector) by swiping with the pointer and pressing the throw button (Space). That way, they can make a new stroke before throwing if they don’t like the previous one.

On the left side of the screen is a rotating big_disk. Add a slider to control the big_disk’s rotation speed.

The big_disk is colored in zones (you can color a distance field from several points), ranging from -1 (blue) to +1 (red).

Pucks remain on the board, and points are tallied at the end.

Important: choose the puck’s mass and the big_disk’s rotation speed so that centrifugal force pushes pucks off the outer 10% of the big_disk’s radius. Friction and static friction should work, with sliders to adjust them; choose suitable values for ice.

The physics must be completely independent of frame rate, with no surface tunneling.

As a puck moves onto the big_disk, it should receive rotational impulse from friction against both the ordinary floor and the big_disk. Think carefully about the correct direction of these forces.

Since the pucks are round, simply calculate the collision time.
