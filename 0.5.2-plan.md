# 0.5.2 plan — shift aim 4× faster, and a time-warp that compares honestly

Two changes: one small and gated by `experiments/client-smoke.js`, one under the gate
**Δwins > 0 on classic ×24** (`experiments/warp-wins.js`, seeds `20261016`, table
`keeper,hunter,balanced`, the baseline being the run recorded in
`archive/0.5.1-worklog.md`).

## 1 Shift: aim gain 4× the speed's

- `js/cfg.js`: `preciseGain: 0.03125` stays the speed gain; new `preciseAimGain: 0.125`
  is the aim gain (4× the speed's, 1/8 of plain instead of 1/32).
- `js/game.js`: `setStroke` uses `preciseGain` for the speed slope and `preciseAimGain`
  for `angDelta`; `nudge` uses the speed gain for ↑/↓ and the aim gain for ←/→. The
  anchor/rebase machinery is untouched (it never depended on the value), so the stroke
  still never jumps when shift is pressed or released.
- `index.html`: the controls line says what the two gains are (speed 32× finer, aim 8×).
- `experiments/client-smoke.js`: the aim gates assert `CFG.preciseAimGain`, the speed
  gates keep `CFG.preciseGain`.

## 2 Warp: three things decide a line — when the search stops, how a position is read, and what the answers are

`js/warp.js`, `js/bots.js`, `js/game.js`. The shortlist (`makeStrokeRank`) and the
settings (`warp`, `warpTries`, `warpTurns`, `warpGoal`, `warpFrameMs`) stay as they are.

### 2a The accept rule: compare the shortlist in full, gate the ladder with the goal

- Candidates are two phases. **Phase 1, the shortlist:** every ranked line from
  `js/bots.js` (up to `warpTries`) is played out and the best kept. No early stop: a bar
  that ends the comparison makes the search identical to the greedy pick on the turns
  where greedy already looks good, and on the rest it shops only where greedy looks bad.
  **Phase 2, the ladder:** only if the best ranked line is below `warpGoal` of one ball's
  annuity, the remaining tries are perturbations around *that* winner (not around the
  greedy line); a perturbation that clears the bar ends the search.
- `begin()` returns false (throw the greedy line) only when there is nothing to compare:
  `warpTries < 2`, or an empty rank. `warpTries: 1` therefore still means exactly the
  greedy line, and the greedy line is always candidate 1, so the pick can never be worse
  than the bot's own choice *on the ruler the search uses*.
- The status line reports the search: `time-warp: 6 of 6 lines played out · best +0.62 of
  a ball (line 3) · good enough | best of them`. The panel's third slider is named for
  what it now does — *extra look under N balls*.

### 2b The ruler: a rollout end is read on the seat's own value function

- `positionValue(w, score, seat, future)` is the same shape as `js/bots.js`
  `evaluateStroke`, read off a board instead of off a prediction: banked score and the
  annuity still coming on the seat's own side (its `score` and `opportunity` weights), the
  mean enemy on its `denial` weight, pucks lost to a hole priced by `lifePrice` with the
  profile's `risk`/`kill`, pucks the recycle rule will return priced by `recPrice` with
  `recovery`/`reserve` and the seat's urgency, all divided by the profile's `opportunity`
  so that one ball is one parked puck's annuity for every profile.
- `recoverableCount` moves to `js/predict.js`'s exports (it already existed there).
- `run.ball` stays the generic ball annuity: `warpGoal` and every status figure stay in
  the same units whatever seat is looking ahead.

### 2c The answers: the copy plays the light search, not a policy that cannot simulate

- `js/bots.js`: `chooseStroke(name, w, cfg, i, pred, rng, roster, rank, light)`. `light`
  samples every other aim level, every other feed radius and angle, and skips the fine
  patch: the same policy, the same predictor, the same economics, ~35 ms a decision
  against ~127 ms. `chooseStrokeQuick`, `spotRate` and `FEED_LOCK` are removed — the quick
  policy's strokes were all thrown at the speed that *stops* on their target, so its
  answers never knocked a parked puck off the disk, and a search priced against them
  learned to leave pucks where a real answer takes them.
- `js/game.js`: a copy's seat answers with `chooseStroke(..., light)`.
- Cost follows: a try is a turn's physics plus two answers, so a searching throw is
  ~1 s of held board at the 6-try/3-turn defaults. The frame-slice gate in
  `experiments/time-warp.js` is restated as what it is — a slice is one frame of the copy
  plus at most one answer, both measured — instead of a hand-picked millisecond figure.

## 3 Measurements

- `experiments/warp-wins.js 24 20261016 classic keeper,hunter,balanced` — the gate; the
  `none` variant must reproduce the 0.5.1 numbers bit for bit (same seeds, warp off).
- `experiments/warp-replies.js [boards] [schema] [table] [turns]` — **new**. Prices the
  planner's shortlist twice: in the planner's own rollouts (`W.tryOne`) and with every
  later seat playing the real search, on a ruler the experiment states itself. It reports
  Kendall's τ between the two orderings, whether the rollout picks the game's own best
  line, and what its pick is worth over the greedy line on the game's ruler. This is the
  cheap instrument that decides whether a reply policy can rank lines usefully before a
  `warp-wins` run is spent on it.
- `experiments/time-warp.js` — the planner's gates, restated for the new rule: every
  ranked line is played (`warpGoal: 0` skips the ladder, not the comparison), a goal out
  of reach buys `min(warpTries, lines + WARP_LADDER.length)` tries, and a line costs the
  same measured or searched.
- `experiments/client-smoke.js` — the browser layers, including the precision block and a
  warped 4-seat game.
