KeRot — rotating disk curling

Every seat gets 4 pucks and pushes one per turn. Pucks the disk can hold (inside the dashed eject ring) ride it around and pick up its spin — the 6-sector face shows each puck's rotation — the rest are flung onto the cold floor.

controls

  • press or drag on the ice outside your puck to aim. The faint ring marks where power starts building. Release a drag to keep the aim without throwing.
  • tap or click your puck once to throw the aimed shot. The Throw button and Space also throw · Esc clears aim · R resets
  • hold shift while dragging for fine control: speed 32× finer, aim 8×
  • ← → nudge the aim, ↑ ↓ the speed (shift = fine there too)
  • when not aiming, swipe right for rules or left for the menu
  • after the last throw, double-click the board (or press R) to deal a new game

score

From the push, each seat earns dt × the red area of the big disk its pucks cover while the board resolves; the red field rotates with the disk. Scores are shown normalised to 0–999 per game, so the number means the same thing for 2 seats and for 8.

"Red" is the red channel of the paint: blue pays about a fifth, pale and red pay nearly the same — the legend under disk pattern has the numbers.

disk pattern

soft lobes (the original), 3 red circles of different sizes, or 6 rim hemispheres — the richest red sits at the rim, right where pucks are flung off. The pattern is the score map, so it can be switched mid-game.

puck mass

Ice grips a heavy puck less: friction falls as mass−1/3. Heavier pucks slide farther and are flung off sooner — the eject ring shrinks — while light ones stop short and stick. auto-ω re-solves the 90 % rule for the current mass. The slider acts on every puck on the board.

return pucks

The shaded eject fan and the wedge + lane rule return spent pucks to their owner's stock — pick one in the side panel.

disk speed in play

With a vote rule on, the seats set the disk speed during the game. Every seat holds one vote, settable at any time: don't care (the default — it abstains), keep the current speed, or a value on the slider — a change of up to ±0.5 rad/s, or an exact speed. The vote closes 1 s after the turn timer, between two turns (every turn) or before the first seat throws again (every round); the average of the votes cast is the new speed, and every vote goes back to don't care. Bots vote too — their votes show next to their scores.

The disk is a motor: it takes 1 s to reach a new speed, and a rider needs friction for that speed-up or slow-down as well, so a big change can shed pucks that a steady disk would keep. The speed bar under the transport buttons sets the speed directly with its ω slider (and auto-ω solves it for the puck mass), and a new game starts from the speed the slider set. Its speed select is the same rule as the radios in the drawer, and your vote row sits right under it.

black holes

The two half-circles above and below the disk are black holes: entering one permanently removes a puck.

bots

Try the retriever, black-hole, or balanced bots to compare recovery and attack strategies. A seat can be switched between human and bot at any time.

speed play

With a vote rule on, the bots do not merely tolerate the disk speed, they play it. read its own board (the default) weighs a speed by what the board pays for it: the riders a ramp would keep or shed, and the room the coming throws get. park leans on that balance and keeps the disk slow, so a push has something to land on and a rider stays where it is; shake leans the other way and runs the disk up to throw the other seats' riders off it; tempo swings with the throw order — down before its own push, up to answer someone else's. never touch the speed leaves the voting to the others.

time-warp

On, a bot plays its turn out in a copy of the game before the real board commits to it: it throws a candidate stroke, lets that turn and the answers to it play out, and reloads the copy for the next one. Every line of its own ranking is played out — at most those lines per turn, a few turns each — and the best of them is what it throws, so looking ahead never plays a worse line than the bot's own pick. If the best is still worth less than so many balls more than the position it started from, the tries that are left go to small changes of that winner, and the first of them to clear the bar ends the search. The board holds still while it looks, and the only thing that reaches the real game is the stroke that survived. These are the game's own rules and not a model of them: a look-ahead of one turn plays the turn bit for bit as the board would (measured in experiments/time-warp.js).

view

fit view (the default) gives the whole screen to the board and keeps the panel behind the ☰ tab on the right edge; the throw and speed bars stay at the top of that panel, the speed one with its ω readout and auto-ω beside it. The view select on the panel switches to the side layout, which trades board size for the settings staying in sight. Either is a URL key: view=fit or view=side.

colour scheme

classic, or kerot-s: navy ice and cream pucks whose centre gauge and tag show the share of the best possible rate each puck is earning right now.

glass presentation

plain is the shipped board. glass ice adds baked frost grain, soft shadows under the disk and every puck, and glassy rims; the field paint sits under a few centimetres of ice, so its edges are scattered, it loses contrast, and air, frost and the blurred mirror of the ceiling lamps lie above it. ice / glass sound adds a sliding ice hiss, bright clinks for puck contacts, duller taps for rails and low swishes for black holes — all synthesised, with no audio files, and every seat colour rings at its own note. The field paint stays the score map, and every seat ring, number and gauge stays readable. The heavy layer is one optional script loaded only when one of these is switched on, so a plain link downloads none of it. Sound waits for a click or key because browsers block audio before a gesture. The crystal ice preset turns both on; fx=glass and sfx=1 are the link keys.

presets and links

Touch a control while the drawer is open and the board on screen is the board you play: settings take effect at once, and ☰ slides them away again.

The preset select applies a named bundle of settings — physics, disk pattern, return rule, speed rule, colour scheme, presentation, the table itself — and starts a fresh board with it. Touch any control afterwards and you have left the bundle, and the select clears. Picking a preset also puts its link into the address bar, so what you see is what you can share.

The same settings are URL parameters: ?preset=stones, or by hand ?schema=rim6&seats=human,hunter,keeper&mass=8&omega=1.2. A named preset applies first and the rest override it, and the link is kept as you typed it — including those overrides — so a refresh opens the same board. Touch a control and the address bar switches to the settings that differ from the shipped table, which is the link of what you see now. preset, omega, muk, mus, mass (kg), schema (classic | circles | rim6), recycle (none | zones | advance), vote (off | turn | round), votemode (delta | exact), theme (classic | kerot), fx (plain | glass), sfx (0|1), botspeed (auto | park | shake | tempo | still), warp (0|1), warptries, warpturns (0 = to the end of the game), warpgoal, seats (a comma list of seat types), balls, ring, ghost (0|1). Names are case-insensitive. Unknown keys are ignored and numbers are clamped to the slider ranges — a hand-typed link always opens.
