# 0.5.2 draft — a coarser fine aim, and a search that compares

User prompts for this round:

```
1
shift drag mode force is ok but angle need be not so slowdown but 4x faster change then now

2
Suggested next step (logged in the worklog): fix the rollout policy or the accept rule — e.g.
always play out the ranked shortlist and take the best — with the gate being Δwins > 0 on
classic ×24 rather than the planner's own ruler.
```

## Reading of the prompts

- **The two halves of shift were one number, and only one of them was wrong.** 0.5.1
  made shift 1/32 on both axes at once, from one `preciseGain`. Speed is the axis that
  needs the leash: a stroke is thrown from a fixed spot at a lobe, and a few cm/s is the
  difference between a lock and an ejection — so the prompt leaves the speed alone. An
  angle is not the same quantity: the disk has 6 lobes, the neighbour of a target is ~60°
  away, and at 1/32 a drag that crosses one lobe wants about a metre of hand travel. The
  prompt asks for 4× on the angle and nothing on the speed, which is two gains where
  there was one, not a new mechanism.
- **The warp verdict is measured, and the worklog's own next step says what to do with
  it.** 0.5.1 ran the look-ahead through `experiments/warp-wins.js` — the real game, same
  seeds, one searcher per variant — and found it does not win more: 15 of 18 cells were
  zero or negative wins, and the one large score gain (random +163) still won nothing. Its
  two candidate mechanisms are both in the accept rule: the bar is the *snapshot*, and the
  first candidate is the greedy line, so the search finishes after one try whenever greedy
  looks good; and when it does compare, it reads its lines against quick-policy replies
  that are weaker than the bots it actually faces.
- **"Always play out the ranked shortlist and take the best" is a change of one rule.**
  The shortlist exists (`makeStrokeRank`: one best stroke per line of play, deduped) and
  the search already receives it. What changes is what the search does with it: the
  comparison is the point, so every line in it gets played, and the accept rule stops
  deciding *whether* to compare. `warpGoal` does not disappear — it becomes the bar for
  the *extra* work, the perturbation ladder around the winner.
- **The gate is wins, not the planner's own ball annuities.** A self-report is not
  evidence (0.5.1's whole point), and the planner's ruler is optimistic by construction
  while its rollouts answer with a cheaper policy than the game plays. The number that
  settles a candidate fix is Δwins on classic ×24 with the same seeds as before, with the
  previously recorded run as the baseline.
- **"Fix the rollout policy or the accept rule" is a choice, and the measurement makes it
  for you.** Doing the accept rule first is what the prompt suggests, and it is cheap —
  but if the fix is only half of it, the other half is a policy, and the way to tell is to
  price the same shortlist twice, once with the planner's answers and once with the
  game's. That is `experiments/warp-replies.js`, written for this round, and what it found
  is in the plan: the accept rule alone leaves the ranking tuned to answers the seat will
  not get.

## Decisions

- **Two gains, one per axis.** `preciseGain` (0.03125) keeps the speed; `preciseAimGain`
  (0.125) is the aim. The drag and the arrow nudge both read the pair, so a shift arrow's
  1° step becomes 0.125° and a shift drag turns 4× the angle it did — the aim is now 8×
  finer than a plain drag instead of 32×, which is what "not so slowdown" asks for.
- **The shortlist is compared in full, whatever the goal is.** Every ranked line is
  played out, up to `warpTries`; the best of them is what the search would take if it
  stopped there. An early exit inside this phase is the bug the measurement found: it
  made the search identical to the greedy line on most turns (6-seat boards: every search
  took line 1) and on the rest it shopped only where greedy already looked bad.
- **`warpGoal` gates the ladder, not the comparison.** If the best ranked line already
  clears `warpGoal` of one ball's annuity, the search is done — the seat has what it asked
  for. If it does not, the remaining tries go to perturbations around that winner (the
  ladder, centred on the winner instead of on the greedy line), and the first
  perturbation that clears the bar ends the search.
- **A rollout is read on the seat's own ruler.** `positionValue` now weights the position
  by the terms of the profile whose line it is judging — score and opportunity on its own
  side, denial on the enemies' — plus the ball terms the same profile prices a stroke with
  (a puck gone to a hole, a puck the recycle rule will return), and divides by the
  profile's opportunity weight so that "one ball" means one parked puck's annuity for a
  keeper (opportunity 1.2) and for a random seat (0.1) alike. A search that re-ranks every
  profile's lines on one generic share makes every profile play the same style; measured,
  the most style-bound profile was the one the look-ahead cost the most wins.
- **The copy's seats answer with a light search, not with a policy that cannot simulate.**
  `chooseStrokeQuick` is gone: its strokes were all thrown at the speed that *stops* on
  their target, so a rollout's answers never knocked a parked puck off the disk, and a
  search priced against them learned to leave pucks where a real answer takes them. The
  copy now answers with `chooseStroke(..., light)`: the same policy, the same simulation,
  the same economics, at every other aim level and every other feed, and without the fine
  patch — 30.7 ms an answer against 121 ms for the full search, measured on the boards the
  ranking diagnostic uses. The ranking it buys is positive but not yet enough to win: on 48
  boards walked in from fresh games the light ruler orders the shortlist much as the game
  does (τ +0.378, positive on 40 of 48) while its pick is worth nothing measurable over the
  greedy line (−0.114 of a ball, se 0.089, ceiling +0.404). The numbers, and the ×24 wins
  gate that failed with them, are in `archive/0.5.2-worklog.md`.
- **The cost is a think, not a stutter, and it is the answers that cost it.** A frame
  slice is still bounded — by one frame of the copy and at most one answer — and where the
  frames are cut still cannot change the pick, but the look-ahead is now ~1 s of held
  board per searching throw at the 6-try/3-turn defaults (measured in
  `experiments/time-warp.js`). The settings that buy it back are `warpTries` (each line is
  a try) and `warpTurns`; both are the user's, and the look-ahead ships off.

## Not in this round

- **The rollout's own later throws are still light.** With `warpTurns: 3` on a 3-seat
  table the horizon is the seat's throw and the two answers to it, so the searcher's own
  next throw is outside it — but on a 2-seat table (or at a longer horizon) the copy plays
  the searcher's own later turns with the light search too, and a line that is good
  because the seat will follow it up is priced by an approximation of the seat.
- **The horizon is still turns, not until the position settles.** A line whose pucks are
  still sliding when the rollout ends is read on the stable rates of the last frame; the
  alternative (play each line out to the board settling) is a different cost class.
- **One reply budget for every seat.** The light search's sampling is a constant
  (`step` 2); whether the *first* answer (the seat that throws next) deserves more samples
  than the second is not measured.
