# 0.5 worklog — speed play, and a bot that looks before it throws

Reference: `0.5-draft.md`, `0.5-plan.md`.

## Review of the previous pass

`archive/0.1.8-worklog.md` closed the speed-vote rule question with a measurement:
`turn-delta` votes 17.3 times a game and moves ω to a mean 1.090, `round-delta` 4.8 times
and 0.776 — the rule decides how often the disk is argued about, and the round rule's
majority-vs-mean-enemy form is the one that does not gang up on the next thrower. Two
things in that pass were left for this one:

- **The rule was settled, the *policy* inside it was not.** `speedWorth` had one set of
  weights for every seat, so the only choice a bot had was the one the arithmetic made for
  it. That is what the first prompt of this round asked for, and it landed as
  `SPEED_STRATS` — the same price function with its five terms exposed (§1 of the plan).
- **Its rim-capture note was a measurement invitation, and it was taken.** The 0.4.1
  worklog ended with "one disk phase in six still parks the rim feed on a blue gap … the
  missing throw is a neighbouring radius or a slightly different arrival time, not a new
  search". Measured here for the policy that cannot simulate (§Bots below): the lock band
  is phase-independent (the disk is rotationally symmetric) and the *paint* is not, so the
  fix is to price each locking radius as the paint will look when the throw arrives — five
  (radius, speed) pairs measured to lock 12/12 phases on all three schemas, and the
  reddest of them thrown. Nothing had to be added to the search grid for it.

## Speed play

- `js/vote.js`: `SPEED_STRATS` (auto, park, shake, tempo, still), `speedStrat(cfg, name)`,
  and an optional strategy argument on `gatherRiders`, `throwWeight` and `chooseVote`. The
  `still` row is `{ none: true }` and returns before the board is read, which is why the
  `move` reluctance, the `room` weight and the `next` term are the whole of the rest.
- `index.html` has a *bot brain* fieldset: the `speed play` select (one line of label per
  strategy: *park — keep the disk slow*), the look-ahead checkbox, its three sliders, and a
  status line the panel refreshes with the rest of the readout. `?botspeed=` takes the name.
- Presets `speedplay` (vote every turn, seats that park) and `foresight` (the look-ahead
  open) ship as the two demonstrations, and `botspeed` / `warp*` keys are in
  `parseQuery`, `applyCfg`, `presetQuery` and `NUM_RANGES` like every other setting.

What it does to a table. Six games per row, `keeper,hunter,balanced`, classic, rotating
table, `node experiments/vote-rules.js 6 classic keeper,hunter,balanced round-delta
auto,park,shake,tempo,still` (kept in
`experiments/results/vote-rules-classic-keeper-hunter-balanced-round-delta-auto-park-shake-tempo-still.json`):

|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| auto | 3.8 | 3.3 | 0.811 | 1.00 | 360 | 425 | 394 |
| park | 3.7 | 2.7 | **0.721** | 0.83 | 397 | 423 | 478 |

Which is the table the names describe: a disk voted to *park* on runs slower than the set
speed, holds what it parks, and outscores the plain economics on two of the three seats (478
against 394 for the balanced one); *shake* runs it faster and sheds half again as many
riders, *tempo* moves the disk on every vote it takes (4.3 of 4.3) and swings
widest — mean 0.977, range 0.42…1.49 — at the lowest scores, because a disk that is never at
rest is a disk nothing can settle on. `still` is the flat line, and it is mid-table: a disk
nobody moves is at least predictable. Under the *turn* rule the same five rows all score
below `still` (measured the same way under `turn-delta`: the best scoring seat gets 231 with
auto and 185 with shake, against 398 for the hunter who never touched the disk)
— 15 votes a game moving ω to a mean of 1.3 is a lot of disk to keep off the brakes, and
that is the 0.1.8 finding that `round` is the shipped rule, now measured with styles on top
of it instead of in place of it.

The directions the names promise are gated in `experiments/speed-vote.js` on one board
rather than averaged over games: under the round rule, `park` asks −0.35 and `shake` +0.17;
under the turn rule `park` asks for *more* than `auto` (+0.23 against +0.20) because with an
enemy throwing next, weighting room means spoiling the room that enemy has to land on.

## Bots

- `makeStrokeRank` / `rankAdd` / `rankFinish`: a search leaves a ranked shortlist behind —
  one best stroke per group, sorted by value — for a caller that wants to try the
  alternatives. It costs the price of three arrays at setup; the ranking itself is an
  insertion into a table of six. `makeStrokeRank` fills `value` with −Infinity so a rank
  nobody filled holds no candidate.
- `chooseStroke(profile, w, cfg, i, pred, rng, roster, rank)` takes the rank as an optional
  eighth argument, so every existing caller (seven experiment scripts) is untouched.
- `chooseStrokeQuick` is the policy a rollout answers with: the same groups, each priced by
  `spotRate` — what a puck *parked on that spot* pays, read through the score's own
  quadrature — led by the throw's flight time so a rim spot is priced as the paint will look
  when the puck arrives. Cost: 0.03 ms a decision against `chooseStroke`'s 190 ms.
- `FEED_LOCK` (the five locking pairs) and a stopped-puck capture test — friction has to
  drag the puck up to `ω d`, over a slide of `(ω d)² / 2 μk g`, and a spot whose slide runs
  past the rim is a puck leaving the disk, not one parked on it; at the defaults that limit
  is the measured ~0.5 R, which is why a rim lobe is a feed or nothing.
- Empty-board first throws, quick policy, 18 phases: classic 18/18 locked at rate 7.8,
  rim6 12/18 at 5.4 (0/18 before the arrival pricing and the locking pairs), circles 4/18
  for the noisy profiles — their own aim jitter walks them off the lock band, a weakness the
  searching profiles share, so the *policy* is not the thing to blame for it.

## Time-warp

`js/warp.js` (a planner owning one copy), the `sim`/flow hooks in `js/game.js`, and the
`warp*` settings. A seat that is about to throw calls `botPrep` (its own search, which also
fills the rank), then hands the rank to the planner and, if there is more than one line to
look at, answers `planning` while `js/main.js` holds the board. `stepPlan` finishes the
search, prints its one line of status and commits the winning stroke.

Cost, node, sparse 6-seat board, per try of a rollout (from the first measurements and
reproduced by the gate at 8.7–14.6 ms/try at a 3-turn horizon):

| `warpTurns` | 1 | 2 | 3 | 0 (to the end) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| ms per try | 17.4 | 43.1 | 14.3 | 73.1 |

A whole game (15 throws, all seats searching, 6 tries each) costs ~330 ms per throw, of
which ~190 ms is the greedy search the page already pays; ~118 of its 4607 frames are the
board standing still. `warpTries: 6` at a 3-turn horizon is therefore ~90 ms of held
board — a think, not a stutter. `warpTurns: 0` is the expensive one (6 tries ≈ 0.44 s) and
is left reachable rather than recommended.

Calibration of the goal, ten mid-game seats, mean tries used and mean gain over the greedy
line, in ball annuities: `0 → 1.0 tries`, `0.3 → 1.5`, `0.5 → 2.5`, `0.8 → 4.0`,
`1.2 → 4.5`, `2 → 5.0`, unreachable → all 6. The gains themselves run p50 0.62 / p90 1.57
on contested boards and 0.00 on quiet ones, so the shipped default is `warpGoal: 0.5`:
a seat in trouble searches, a seat with nothing to complain about throws at once. (The
first candidate is always the greedy line, so a bar of 0 means "take it" — the slider's
zero is labelled *the best of them* for the same reason.)

## What the gates found, and what it cost to fix

Four bugs, all of them caught by `experiments/time-warp.js`, none visible on the page:

1. **A rollout inherited the previous rollout's votes.** `clearVotes` ran when the copy's
   roster was fitted, not when a line was launched, so candidates 2…6 of a search started
   from a game in which somebody had already voted — worth a different disk speed, and
   enough to make the same line measure 0.3 of a ball differently inside one search. The
   gate is "a line costs the same measured or searched".
2. **A copy's later seats never threw.** The tick guard required `aim.hasStroke`, which only
   the *live* aim ever sets, so every rollout after the first turn ran to the step fuse:
   214 ms a try instead of 14. Fixed by making the guard `(!sim && !aim.hasStroke)`, so a
   copy throws the moment it is armed — and the fuse is now a safety net rather than the
   usual exit.
3. **Two games in one process steered each other's disk.** `closeVote` writes `cfg.omega`
   on the module `CFG` every game shares, so a harness that built a second `createGame({})`
   contaminated the first (a false PARITY FAIL). A copy runs on its own cfg; a harness game
   has to as well.
4. **`bots.js` answers with one shared scratch record.** `chooseStroke` returns `botOut`, so
   a caller that keeps "the greedy line" for later was really keeping the *last* line
   anyone asked for — which is how the calibration read +0.30 of improvement for a search
   that had taken the greedy line unchanged. The harness copies the stroke it keeps; the
   page's own callers read it before anything else can ask.

Also fixed while measuring: the plan was cancelled by `!playsItself()` before its first try
if a seat ran out of balls mid-search, which is correct, and `cancelPlan` on a `setWarp`
change, so switching the look-ahead off mid-think throws the pending search away instead of
honouring it.

## Next step suggestion

Measure the look-ahead in wins, not in its own units. Its 0.5-round evidence is the
planner's own value function (a search beats the greedy line by p50 0.62 of a ball *as the
planner prices it*), and its rollouts face a policy that is weaker than the searching bots
the real game plays — so the number is an upper bound on the benefit. Run
`experiments/compete.js` and `experiments/bot-strategies.js` with `warp` on and off on all
three schemas, same seeds, and read the scores: if a searching bot does not win more, the
rollout policy or the accept rule is what to fix, not the search. `bot-strategies.js` at 4
seeds takes over 15 minutes per variant — start it before anything else in that round, and
if a cheaper cut is wanted, `experiments/matchup.js` measures per-seat disk time and locks,
which is where a better line would have to show up.
