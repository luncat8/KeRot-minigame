# 0.1.5 worklog — contact integration, turn handoff timer, zone outline

Reference prompt: `0.1.5-draft.md`. Fixes three reported issues on top of 0.1.4
(`d88c3e0`), two of them in physics and one in the renderer.

## Review of the previous task (0.1.4) — what was actually wrong

0.1.4 was accepted as a fix for the partially-intersected case. It was not one. Its
contact model sampled **one patch at a time** — a disk patch at the overlap centroid
and a floor patch at the centre of the uncovered cap — and split the friction budget
between them by area. What that does to the reported case, measured from the committed
build:

| scenario (60 s, h = 1/480) | 0.1.4 (`d88c3e0`) | continuum reference |
|---|---|---|
| rest on the rim, a ≈ 0.49 | **frozen on the spot: sleeps at t = 0.00 s** | rolls 4 cm, sleeps 6.4 s |
| rest 5 cm onto the disk, a ≈ 0.8 | sleeps 3.83 s (peak \|ω\| 0.74) | sleeps 4.25 s (peak 2.40) |
| slow slide 0.02 m/s on the rim | frozen at t = 0.10 s | rolls, sleeps 6.4 s |
| slow shake on the rim | **never settles — 60 s** | sleeps |

Two defects fall out of the decomposition:

1. **A straddling puck cannot roll.** The disk's drag is applied at the overlap
   centroid, so it levers the puck — but the companion couple is saturated against the
   same area share, and the eps-snap's second clause (`a ≤ 1 − a`, meant as "the floor
   grip out-holds the disk drive on a jammed puck") declares any straddler of a ≤ ½
   asleep the moment it is slow. A rest straddler is frozen the frame it is touched,
   instead of rolling; 0.1.4's gate measured the "fix" as "puck sleeps", which is what
   a freeze looks like, and the deeper straddle (a ≈ 0.8) escapes the clause but takes
   the other route to a wrong answer (peak 0.74 rad/s against the reference's 2.40).
2. **The last few centimetres never end.** The shake case slides under `epsSleep` and
   neither the drag nor the snap resolves it: 60 s of simulation, no sleep, so the turn
   cannot advance — the same "long contact" the user reported.

(The first attempt at a fix in this round kept the decomposition but made the patch
lever follow the slip and gave the couple its own budget. That pair cross-couples
through ρ and is *not* dissipative: a rest straddler on the turning disk wound up to
|ω| ≈ 9 ω_disk and walked 11.75 m along the rim, never sleeping. It is recorded here
because it is the trap this case sets: any decomposition that adds a lever and a couple
by hand can pump energy, and a gate written from the model will not see it.)

The continuum reference settled it. `/tmp/kr/exact2.js` integrates the exact Coulomb
law over 160² cells per puck (all-kinetic, uniform pressure, disk turning under the
covered cells, h = 1/480): a rest straddler **rolls** a couple of centimetres and then
*settles* — d = 0.842 (a ≈ ½) and 0.897 (a ≈ 0.8), peak |ω| = 1.67 and 2.40 rad/s on
the way. A squeezed puck rolls between the rim and the ice like a wheel, so it does
briefly turn faster than the disk. (An earlier coarse-grid version of the same
reference, quoted in 0.1.3's worklog, was misread: its reported instability came from
holding the disk fixed while the puck slid across it.)

## Implementation

### 1. Contact integration (`js/physics.js`)

`dragPatch()` is gone. `applyFriction()` now integrates the friction of the whole puck
per step over a fixed pattern — 4 rings × 16 rays, equal weights, radii
`√((k+½)/4)` rescaled so `Σw|ρ| = (2/3)r³` — and each sample rubs against its own
surface:

- a sample under the disk rubs against the disk's surface velocity `ω_disk ẑ×ρ_abs`,
- the rest against the still floor,
- each cell's Coulomb force opposes *its own* slip, `−μk·g·(w/N)·û`.

So a partially intersected puck gets the disk's drag, the floor's drag and the torque,
from the same law and the same budget: dissipative by construction, power
`Σ −μk·g·(w/N)·|u| ≤ 0` every step. The pattern reproduces two closed forms exactly,
which is why those numbers are chosen: `Σw = 1` makes a translating puck feel exactly
μk·g, and `Σw|ρ| = (2/3)r³` makes a pure spin on still ice decay at exactly
`spinDrag·μk·g/r`. Force-matched against a 40×160 quadrature to 0.0 % and torque to
within 2 %; an *off-disk* force matches to rounding, a straddle's spin decay to 1e-12.

What this changes physically, all of it in the direction of the reference:

- a fast crossing is nearly **torque-free** (slip is almost uniform under the puck, the
  torque integral cancels): a 2.4 m/s throw now picks up peak |ω| ≈ 0.70 rad/s instead
  of the saturated couple's 0.94, and it turns the way the disk turns;
- a straddler rolls because the two surfaces' drags pass on opposite sides of its
  centre — the moment is not added by hand, it is what the integral gives;
- **the wind-up is gone**: the two rim scenarios now settle at d = 0.842 / 0.896 and
  peak at 1.70 / 2.42 rad/s, versus the reference 0.842 / 0.897 and 1.67 / 2.40.

### 2. Sleep gate (`js/physics.js`)

The eps-snap survives — friction alone jitters around zero forever — and its test now
asks whether the *floor patch alone* can hold the disk patch's whole kinetic drive:
force against `μs(1−a)` and the moment of that force against the floor patch's own
moment capacity `μs(1−a)λ`, normalised as `(f/cap)² + (m/cap·λ)² ≤ 1`.

- A sliver of overlap (a ≈ 0.018) is held: the puck stops, the round ends.
- A half-overlapped puck on the turning disk (a ≈ 0.49) is not: it is still being
  driven, so it keeps sliding — this is exactly the case whose absence let the old
  snap freeze a straddler mid-drag.

No roll threshold, no `muRoll`, no `epsStick`: the flip-flop between "threshold fires and
freezes a dragged puck" and "no threshold and the puck creeps forever" is resolved by
asking who can hold whom.

### 3. Turn handoff timer (`js/game.js`, `js/cfg.js`, `js/ui.js`)

`CFG.handoffT = 4` splits the two jobs `maxResolve` had. The resolve now ends when the
board settles **or** at 4 s, whichever comes first; pucks still moving after the handoff
stay on the board and keep being simulated while the next player aims. `maxResolve` is
only the ghost-re-sim horizon and the competition driver's cap now. The UI state line
shows "resolving… next turn in N s", so the timer is visible in play.

### 4. Zone drawing (`js/render.js`, `js/rules.js`)

Two drawing bugs, both visible in `experiments/logs/zone-before-after.png`:

- `bakeZone()` punched the **full** guard circle out of the wedge as an evenodd hole.
  The circle's part outside the triangle is covered once, so it filled: the teal hatch
  halo hugging the disk in every mode. The hole is now the guard **sector** between the
  wedge's two sides (every zone table puts a vertex on the disk centre, so the wedge's
  interior angle *is* that sector), and the outline strokes the wedge's real boundary —
  two sides, the base, and the closing arc.
- A `line.rect()` in the middle of a stroked path made canvas continue from the rect's
  corner: long stray diagonals across the board in `advance` mode. The outline is now
  one subpath built in order.

`js/rules.js` gained `inGuard()` and now applies it per rule instead of the blanket
`onDisk()` test. For these tables the two are equivalent (`onDisk` is `inGuard` plus
the boundary itself), and `zone-catch` still passes with the wedge+lane share moving
from 23.7 % to 22.6 % because the drawn hole is the tested hole. Fill and rule can no
longer disagree, and nothing extra is drawn that the rule does not do.

`experiments/spin.js` was rewritten against the integral rather than the old patch
decomposition. Its new spec:

- ride spin-up = `spinDrag·μk·g/r` **exactly** on the first step (the classic spinning
  contact, `Σw|ρ| = (2/3)r³`), and force-free while it co-moves;
- straddling a **still** disk is bare ice, exactly: a pure spin decays at
  `−spinDrag·μk·g/r·h`, a pure translation at `−μk·g·h`, force ≈ 0 for the spin;
- friction force matches an independent 40×160 quadrature within 1 %, torque within 5 %;
- off-disk force matches the reference to rounding, spin decay within 3 %;
- straddle settle distance and peak |ω| within 0.01 m / 0.09 rad/s of the 160²
  continuum reference (`straddle d0=0.800: sleep@6.40s d=0.842, peak 1.697` vs

- the older invariants unchanged: ice decay time, lock snap `om === omegaCur`, riding

## Notes for whoever comes next

- The pattern is a fixed quadrature, not adaptive: 4×16 was chosen for exactness at the
  two calibrated limits plus ~2 % torque error in between, at 9.4 µs/step. If a future
  case needs more, add rings — the radii are generated, not hardcoded.
- A lock still snaps the spin onto `ω_disk` on the frame it fires, and the spin ramp
  only shapes behaviour *before* capture. Making the capture respect the ramp needs a
  second state (gripped-but-not-captured), not a gate.
- Tunneling is unchanged: `maxSubsteps` 8 at `h = 1/480` (1.7 cm per substep) has no
  discrete overlap test, so a puck crossing the rim between substeps sees the disk only
  on the steps that sample it. `tunneling-fuzz` covers 50 m/s.
- `experiments/logs/straddle-before-after.png` and `zone-before-after.png` are
  gitignored; regenerate with `/tmp/kr/traj.js` and `/tmp/kr/shot2.js`.
