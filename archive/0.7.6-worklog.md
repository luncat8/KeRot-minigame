# 0.7.6 worklog — Score parts and the pay curve

Two requests, one subject: inspect what the red and blue parts of the big disk pay,
and add settings to view and adjust the curve that decides it. The paint is the score
map (js/field.js) and a seat is paid its red channel (js/score.js), so "inspect the
parts" and "adjust the curve" are the same surface from two sides. Draft:
`archive/0.7.6-draft.md`.

## 0. Review of 0.7.5

Nothing to fix. The autoplay slice budget and the record's once-per-game gate both
held up under this round's instrumentation (the record still steps exactly once per
finished game with three extra `UI.update` calls a frame; the smoke pins it).

## 1. What the parts are

The palette names three parts, and the page already counts them that way:
**red** = field v ≥ 0.5, **pale** = |v| < 0.5, **blue** = v ≤ −0.5 — the same cut
`experiments/field-preview.js` prints area shares at (classic: 14 % red, 54 % pale,
31 % blue). The first attempt split at v = 0 ("the field's sign") and told a useless
story: on `classic` 84 % of the disk sits above 0, so "red vs blue" read 84/16 while
the board shows two cold lobes and a hot one. The colour cut matches what a player
sees, so the parts are the paint's three colours.

## 2. The curve (`js/field.js`, `js/cfg.js`)

`fieldColor` is now the pay curve proper: the shipped two-anchor palette becomes

	pay(v) = pale + (anchor − pale) · |v|^payBend

with `CFG.payBlue` the blue endpoint (the red channel at v = −1) and `CFG.payBend` the
exponent; `CFG.payBend = 1`, `payBlue = 55` reproduce the shipped palette bit for bit
(curve.js gate 2, exact). G and B run on the same bent curve, so the blue→pale→red
path never changes hue, only where along v it sits. Monotone for every allowed
setting — bend ≥ 0.25 and payBlue ≤ pale(245) keep the red channel rising with v —
and the ceiling at v = +1 stays 255, so `RED_MAX` and `scoreRef` are untouched and
bot behaviour at the default setting is byte-identical.

## 3. The split (`js/score.js`)

- One bake, three grids: `bakeScoreLUT` fills the pay grid plus its masks to the red
  and the blue part (`v ≥ 0.5` / `v ≤ −0.5`), keyed with the curve knobs in `lutKey`.
  The inspector reads the masks; the pale pay is `total − red − blue`, so nothing is
  integrated twice and `red + pale + blue = rate` exactly (curve.js gate).
- `puckSplit` re-walks the score's own sample pattern over the three grids (the lens
  over the rim); `seatSplit` sums a seat; `diskPaintStats` reads the bake's node
  counts — area and pay shares of all three parts, cached per paint. This runs at
  readout rate (~5 Hz), never per frame: the paying pass stays single-grid, measured
  back at 930 ns/puck (budget 2 ms; the bake grew one pass over two more arrays).
- The masked grids read as zero outside their part, so a puck straddling a colour
  edge quantises its split by the 1.25 cm bake cell — the paid total is smooth over
  the same edge, the split is not. Gated at 10 % of the rate on the disk, 1 % of a
  full puck over the rim (curve.js); the totals themselves stay at scoring.js's 1 %.

## 4. The settings (`index.html`, `js/ui.js`)

A **pay curve** fieldset under the disk pattern: the curve canvas (the paint itself,
one column per pixel, with the pay curve traced over it and a dashed line at the
colour cut), the part-shares line, and the two sliders. The canvas redraws on demand
only (slider input, ~5 Hz update, size change) and is skipped entirely in the node
stub (`getContext` missing). Readouts are throttled through a signature like the
panel's other writers. The live score line splits the turn seat's earning by part;
the final breakdown shows what each finished seat holds and from which part.

URL keys `payblue`, `paybend` (clamped to the ranges, case-insensitive like the
rest); the sliders echo them into a custom link; the default preset restores 55/1;
the board editor's copy-preset carries them in its setup; the random shuffle draws
the curve (weighted toward the shipped one: 55/1 three of five draws, bends 0.6/1.8,
blues 120/200/240), so a shuffled game can play a genuinely different economy.
Renderer rebake conditions (js/render.js, js/fx-glass.js) include the curve knobs,
so paint and score can never drift apart.

## 6. Next step suggestion

The curve view is static per paint; the natural next step is drawing the *board's*
pay on it — the three parts' shares as bars under the curve, and a puck's covered
area as a marker where it sits — so a player can read "this seat holds blue" without
the table. If the curve is meant to be fought over, a seat-specific tint (each seat's
covered parts in his colour under the curve) would make the speed vote readable too.
