# 0.5.2 worklog — shift's aim 4× faster, and the look-ahead still does not win

Two asks in one round: shift+drag's speed is the right leash but its angle is not — turn
the angle 4× as far for the same hand travel — and take up the 0.5.1 worklog's own next
step: fix the look-ahead's accept rule or its rollout policy, and hold the fix to
Δwins > 0 on classic ×24 rather than the planner's own ball annuities.

The first is in and gated. The second was built in three steps — the accept rule, the
ruler, the replies — each screened on wins, and the 24-game gate came back **negative**:
the searching keeper loses 4 wins of 24, the other two seats are within noise. The
search's ranking was measured on its own too: on 48 boards the light rollout orders the
shortlist much as the game does (τ +0.38) and still picks a line worth nothing over the
greedy one the seat would have thrown anyway. The look-ahead ships off, as before.

## Review of the previous pass

`archive/0.5.1-worklog.md` measured the look-ahead in wins and found it does not win
more (15 of 18 cells zero or negative), and named two mechanisms, both outside the
search itself: the accept rule stopped comparing as soon as the greedy line cleared a
bar read off the snapshot, and the lines it did compare were priced against
quick-policy replies the real table never gives. Its suggested cut is the one taken
here — always play out the ranked shortlist, keep `warpGoal` as the bar for the
perturbation ladder only — re-measured on the same classic ×24 gate.

## Warp: three fixes, screened

`experiments/warp-wins.js` (classic, keeper,hunter,balanced, seeds `20261016`, one
searcher per variant, flag toggled per turn). Δ is the searching profile against its
own `none` baseline; Δwins in pp, Δscore beside it, 12 games each:

| fix in place | keeper | hunter | balanced |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| accept rule: compare the shortlist in full | −8 pp / +8 | 0 pp / +2 | −8 pp / −6 |
| + a profile-priced rollout ruler | −8 pp / +2 | 0 pp / −15 | −8 pp / +28 |

Each row adds one fix to the row above and re-runs the same seeds. The third row is the one that looked like it
had turned the corner, and it is why the ×24 gate was run before anything shipped. It
did not hold:

| variant, 24 games | Δwins | Δscore |
|---|---:|---:|
| keeper+warp | −17 pp (−4) | −22 |
| hunter+warp | +4 pp (+1) | +15 |
| balanced+warp | −4 pp (−1) | +11 |

`none` reproduced the 0.5.1 numbers bit for bit (302 ± 105, 25 % / 339 ± 120, 38 % /
366 ± 99, 38 %), so the dice are the same ones and the delta is the search. The keeper's
loss is the one cell that looks larger than noise — 2 wins of 24 against 6 (P ≈ 0.04
under a no-effect null); the other two are one win either way. In every variant the
searcher's opponents gain more score than the searcher does (keeper+warp: hunter +52,
balanced +26, keeper −22), and a warped table throws slightly more often (12.4 → 12.5–13.3
throws) — a longer game hands everyone more, so the searcher's share of it does not rise
with its own total.

Cost, same run: 3.65 s/game with nobody searching, 6.5–6.9 s with one searcher (52–56
frames of ~3700 held for a search), 13.0 s with all three. A searching throw is ~1.0 s
of held board at the 6-try/3-turn defaults (`experiments/time-warp.js` prints 971 ms per
throw; `warpTries` and `warpTurns` are the settings that buy it back).

## What the ranking is worth

`experiments/warp-replies.js` (written this round) prices the same shortlist twice — once
through the planner's own rollouts (`W.tryOne`, light replies) and once with every later
seat playing the real search — and scores both on a ruler the experiment states itself:
banked score plus the stable-rate annuity over the game's remaining windows, less the
mean of the same for the enemies. It rewinds the snapshot between lines, one board per
fresh game, a per-board seed for the turns walked in. 48 boards, 6 tries, 3 turns:

- mean τ +0.378, positive on 40 of 48, negative on 7 — the light ruler does order the
  lines the way the game orders them.
- the rollout picks the game's own best line on 20 of 48 (32 in the top two), and the
  line it picks is worth **−0.114** of a ball against the greedy line on the game's
  ruler (sd 0.617, se 0.089 over 48 boards, so not distinguishable from zero), where the
  best line on the board was worth **+0.404**.
- it overrode the greedy line on 23 boards: the game agreed the override was the better
  one on 10, the rollout was optimistic on 13 and pessimistic on 0.
- comparing only the first k lines of the ranking, on the game's ruler: 1 line 0.000
  (the greedy line) · 2 −0.027 · 3 −0.077 · 4 −0.114 · 5 −0.116 · 6 −0.137 — every line
  past the first is worth less than the one before it, so the shortlist comparison as it
  stands pays for nothing it adds.
- an answer costs 30.7 ms light against 121 ms for the full search (4×).

Two lessons live in the same file. The first sample — 5 usable boards, all walked from
one deterministic game — read τ +0.587 and **+0.263** of a ball over greedy; that is the
reading the light replies went in on. Fresh games and per-board seeds move the same
measurement to τ +0.378 and −0.114. And with the pick indistinguishable from greedy
while the board's best line is +0.4 of a ball away, the search's fault is not *when it
stops* — that rule now compares everything — but *what its rollout reads*.

## Next step suggestion

Two candidate cuts, cheapest first, both measurable before a warp-wins run is spent:

1. **Price the replies.** The rollout answers with the light policy (~31 ms) while the
   live table's later seats answer with the full search (~121 ms). Rank the same
   shortlist three ways — light replies, real replies, and the game's own playout — and
   ask whether the real-replies ordering's pick beats the greedy line. If it does, the
   fix is to pay for better answers (or to make the light answer closer to the full one,
   e.g. light for the first later turn and full for the second) and the ×24 gate decides.
   If it does not, the ruler is the suspect, not the answers.
2. **Gate the override on its own margin.** Take a ranked line only when the rollout
   beats greedy by more than the measurement's noise (the 48-board sample puts se ≈ 0.09
   of a ball on that difference). `warp-replies.js` can price "override iff gap ≥ t" for
   a few t on the same boards before any wins run.

If neither move the ×24 gate in a round, the honest home for the look-ahead is the panel
(a visible "what it would have thrown") rather than the bots' policy. The keeper is the
one cell that looks larger than noise (−4 wins of 24, P ≈ 0.04 one-sided, and one of
three cells), so if the next cut is more games rather than another fix, it is the same
3-seat table at ×48 — the harness runs every variant, about half an hour on this box. A
one-profile table is not the shortcut: with one seat it is a solo game and wins are 100 %
by construction (measured: 12 games, 5.3 throws each, Δscore +15 of noise).
