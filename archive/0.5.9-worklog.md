# 0.5.9 worklog — the speed control moves to the throw bar, and a review pass

Scope: (1) disk speed in play is a *game control*, so it moves out of the settings and
up against the transport bar; (2) the prompt's review pass — find and fix issues and
performance problems. Bot balance is out of scope (slow to test), as in 0.5.8.

## 1. The speed bar

Before: the ω slider sat among the disk settings, and the "disk speed in play" group
(rule radios + the vote block, with a second ω slider's worth of vote rows) sat halfway
down the panel. Changing the speed, seeing who sets it, or casting a vote all meant
scrolling.

After: `#speedBar` directly under `#actions` holds everything a turn needs — the speed
rule as a select, the ω slider with its readout and **auto-ω**, and the vote block (a
row per human seat while a vote rule is on). The old group in the settings keeps only
its three rule radios, which are the same setting as the select; the duplicate ω slider
in the settings is gone (`index.html` has exactly one `#omega`, gated by
`client-smoke.js`).

- `js/ui.js`: `SPEED_RULE_LABELS` (`panel ω` / `vote: turn` / `vote: round`) fills the
  select; `setRule(rule, mode)` now drives both the select and the drawer radios, so
  either control moves the other, and drops a held vote when the mode changes (a
  "change of ±0.4" and "an exact 2.2" are different votes). `showVote` writes a standing
  vote back into a *rebuilt* row.
- Bug found while wiring it: after a vote-mode switch the rebuilt row read "no change"
  while the game still held the vote at `+.40` — the row was rebuilt from the mode, not
  from the seat. Fixed in `showVote`; `experiments/client-smoke.js` now rebuilds a row
  (a human seat joins) and checks the new slider shows the held value.
- Bug found in my own layout: auto-ω had been left in `#actions` and hidden by
  `#actions #btnAuto { display: none }` in the fit rail, so on a phone the control
  disappeared entirely. It now lives in the speed bar, which is where the rail's styles
  expected it; the smoke test checks it is not in the transport bar.
- Fit layouts (`index.html` CSS): the 66 px rail stacks the bar as a column (rule
  select, ω readout over a quarter-width slider, auto-ω), the 52 px portrait bar keeps
  it as a row and drops the slider (no room); the drawer keeps the vote rows. Rule
  order matters here — the `#panel:not(.open) #speedBar` rules sit *after* the rail's
  `#panel.open > :not(#actions) { display: block }`, so the bar stays visible on the
  rail instead of being swept away with the other settings.
- Help drawer text follows: the speed bar, its ω slider, auto-ω and the mirroring
  select are described where the old "disk ω in the settings" paragraph was.

## 2. Review: what was found

**Disk texture was repainted on every resize.** `Render.resize()` rebaked the disk
sprite at a resolution derived from the window, so every step of a window drag, a phone
rotation, a drawer opening or a fit-layout switch cost a full field bake. Measured
(headless, `@napi-rs/canvas`): **60 refits across 900→1620 css px cost 3.26 s before,
0.02 s after** — ~54 ms per refit versus ~0.3 ms. The map is now a stepped texture
(128 / 256 / 512 device px per world unit, chosen from the backing-store scale) that is
baked at setup, rebaked once when the surface outgrows a step, and **never shrunk**, so
a phone-sized refit keeps the sharp map a desktop surface bought. A rendering compare
at 1200×733 shows the new map equal to the old window-scaled one at that size (the
first attempt — a flat 256 px map — was visibly soft on a desktop board and is why the
steps exist).

**Ghost prediction simulated past the turn.** `Game.tick` re-simulated the aim preview
to `cfg.maxResolve` (8 s), while the turn is paid by `cfg.handoffT` (4 s). On a crowded
board the tail is most of the cost: **7.1 ms → 3.8 ms per ghost stroke** on a 13-puck
board. The ghost now stops at the handoff window; the bots still price the full
horizon, so their picks are unchanged (verified: identical `duration` and `endX` at the
sampled stroke in `client-smoke.js`).

**Rejected, with the measurement that rejected it.** `js/score.js` was rewritten to
share a `ratesOf()` helper between `scoreRates` and `stableRates` with a lazy "nowhere
near red → 0" skip. Profiling showed `scoreRates` is ~1.5 % of a bot decision, and A/B
runs on identical boards put `chooseStroke` at 143–186 ms/decision in both trees
(within noise, sometimes *slower*). The skip rested on a false premise — `rateOf`
averages the red and bright channels over its sample cells, so red coverage is not
concave in puck position — and a `redMax` upper-bound prune of the stroke grid changed
too many picks to be safe (84 of 840 candidates skipped, different strokes chosen).
Both experiments are reverted; `js/score.js` is untouched this round. The remaining
stall (a bot decision is 145–360 ms at 12–14 pucks, ~half of it `applyFriction` and
`findEvent` inside the rollout) is a physics/bot-search cost, not a bug, and belongs to
the bot-balance work that is out of scope here.

## Next step

The bot's think time is the one real remaining cost (145–360 ms of main-thread work per
bot turn at 12+ pucks, ~45–58 % of it `applyFriction` in the rollout). It needs an
`applyFriction` fast path for a puck nowhere near a contact — sleep states already skip
one, but a *sliding* puck far from every wall, puck and rim still pays the full cell
sweep. That is a physics change with a bot-strength measurement attached
(`experiments/compete.js`, `bot-strategies.js`), so it is its own round.
