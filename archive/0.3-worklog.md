# 0.3 worklog — GUI: fit-to-screen board, help drawer, default table

Reference: `0.3-draft.md` (this round's user prompt).

## Review of 0.2

The 0.2 round (commit `aad635b`) left the board playable with six bot profiles, two
black holes and per-seat reserves. The parts this round touches were its loose ends:
the rules were a 14-line paragraph permanently occupying the top of the side panel; the
canvas was sized `width: 100%` with its height derived from the width, so a short or
narrow window pushed the panel below the fold and grew a page scrollbar; and the default
table was one human against a single `hunter`, which shows none of the strategy spread
the profile work added.

## Implementation

- **`js/help.js` (new)** — the drawer: pointer within `EDGE` = 28 px of the left edge
  opens it, moving past its right edge closes it again. Touch moves are ignored (`hover`
  has no meaning there); instead a swipe in from the edge (`SWIPE` = 48 px, 700 ms) opens
  it, a swipe left closes it. The `?` tab pins it open — pinned, it survives the pointer
  leaving and closes on a tap out on the board, on × or on Esc. Moving the drawer out of
  the panel also let the rules text be split into titled sections (controls, score,
  return, black holes, bots) instead of one paragraph.
- **`index.html`** — `body`/`#app` are now a fixed-height flex row (`100dvh`,
  `overflow: hidden`, `box-sizing: border-box`, safe-area padding); `#boardWrap` is the
  flex item that takes the space and clips; `#panel` keeps a fixed 320 px and scrolls
  inside itself. Under 760 px the row becomes a column with the panel capped at 46% of
  the height. The throw button is the only green control.
- **`js/render.js`** — `resize()` fits the board into its box (largest rect of the board
  aspect, floor to whole pixels, letterboxed by `scale`/`ox`/`oy`), raises the
  device-pixel cap from 2 to 3 with a 6 Mpx backing-store budget, and returns early when
  the size has not changed so an observer firing repeatedly cannot re-bake the textures.
  The puck ring is inset by half its width. The disk is a sprite cut to the circle once
  (`bakeDisk`, `destination-in`) instead of a per-frame circular clip.
- **`js/main.js`** — refits on `resize`, `orientationchange`, a `ResizeObserver` on the
  board box and the visual viewport; binds the drawer.
- **`js/game.js` / `js/ui.js`** — the default table is `human, balanced, blackhole,
  retriever`; a seat added to an all-human table joins as `balanced`.

## Validation

- `experiments/client-smoke.js`: pass. Extended with the fit (aspect kept, box never
  exceeded, tight axis filled, backing store capped in a wide box), the drawer (opens on
  a near-edge pointer, closes when it leaves, pins from the tab, closes on a tap out,
  ignores touch moves, opens on a swipe in, closes on a swipe left) and the new default
  table (`seatTypes` are exactly `human,balanced,blackhole,retriever`). The auto-played
  game now runs four seats and checks the winner as the argmax over all of them.
- Frame diff through `@napi-rs/canvas` (2000×1222 device px, 7 throws on the board):
  **16 217 pixels differ, all of them on a puck rim** — 11 901 inside the new ring and
  4 316 where the old ring's outer half used to bleed over the board. Everything else —
  floor, zones, black holes, disk, ghost, HUD — is byte-identical.
- The pre-cut disk sprite does not leak: sampled outside the circle but inside the
  texture square (1.02 R, 1.10 R, 1.30 R at 45°), before and after agree to the channel
  (`75,90,111`, `74,89,111`, `73,88,111`).
- `experiments/determinism.js`, `energy.js`, `eject-radius.js`, `lock-band.js`,
  `mass-cancellation.js`, `overlap-centroid.js`, `scoring.js`, `settings-revive.js`,
  `spin.js`, `tunneling-fuzz.js`, `zone-catch.js`, `bench.js`: pass.
- Classic-script top-level declaration scan: 117 declarations, no collisions.

## Next step suggestion

Play it on a phone-width window and on a short landscape one — the two shapes the fit
logic has to serve — and check whether the 46% panel share on narrow screens leaves the
board big enough to aim on. If it does not, the panel wants to become a second drawer
rather than a shorter column.
