## Review of the previous task (0.1.3) — what was wrong

0.1.3 dragged a straddling puck by the surface velocity sampled at the patch centroid,
which is right, and then spun it with a saturated couple whose budget was the *area*
share. Three defects fell out of that, all in the partial-intersection case:

1. **No moment arm.** The drag impulse was applied at the puck centre, so a puck
   straddling the rim got the disk's pull but none of its torque: it never rolled
   between the two surfaces, it just acquired the disk's spin. The "puck partially
   intersected with big_disk" case was a *translation* effect only.
2. **The spin budget was the area, not the moment.** Two patches of equal area but very
   different shape (a sliver at the rim vs a cap near the centre) cannot exert the same
   torque; 0.1.3 gave them the same rate. Measured with the disk still — where the disk
   and the floor are the *same* surface, so the answer must equal bare ice — a
   straddling puck's spin decayed 36 % too slowly (0.05163 vs 0.03800 in 0.5 s).
3. **The rim roller never settled.** A puck left straddling the rim crept along it and
   span up: 38 s of simulation, `om = −6.9971`, position (0.957, 1.893), and the round
   only ended because the 8 s resolve cap fired. Checked against exact Coulomb
   quadrature over the contact (`/tmp/kr/exact.js`, 1400² grid, `a = 0.477`): the
   runaway is *real* physics for a pure-μk contact, not a bug — the floor patch drives
   the creep as much as the disk patch drives the spin, and there is no fixed point:

   | `om` | disk patch torque | floor patch torque | net |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | 0.0 | −0.292 | −0.318 | −0.610 |
   | −0.6 | −0.270 | +0.007 | −0.263 |
   | −2.0 | −0.209 | +0.471 | **+0.262** |
   | −5.0 | −0.008 | +0.515 | **+0.507** |

   (units of μk·p·(2/3)πr³; tangential creep `v = 0.06 m/s`). Kinetic friction alone
   cannot stop it; the missing mechanism is static friction at the rolling contact.

## Implementation

### 1. Physics (`js/physics.js`) — one contact, two patches, one budget

- **`dragPatch()` now levers.** The impulse goes on at the patch centroid, so it carries
  `Δω = 2(ρ × Δv)/r²`. The step is capped by the effective mass at the patch,
  `1 + 2(ρ × û)²/r²` — the same moment fed back into the slip. Without that cap the
  lever reverses the slip along û and the contact *injects* energy (measured: a puck
  gaining KE across the rim).
- **The budget is `∫|ρ|dA`, not the area.** `segMoment(r, a)` is the exact polar
  integral of |ρ| over a circular segment (`∫sec³` is elementary); `drillShare()` turns
  the two caps of the lens into `qd` (disk) and `qf` (floor), which partition the puck
  exactly (`qd + qf = 1`). For the full disc the budget is (2/3)μk·m·g·r, i.e.
  `ω̇ = spinDrag·μk·g/r` — the 0.1.3 rate, so the old spin gates still hold. The disk's
  cap is a sliver (`R = 8r`); its mean radius `(r + 2|a1|)/3` matches grid quadrature to
  6.25e-3, against 4.32e-2 for `d − h2` and 2.80e-2 for `|d − h2|`.
- **Sliding and drilling draw on the same budget.** `dragPatch()` returns the angular
  impulse it spent, and the couple gets `max(0, q·budget − spent)`. A puck rolling on
  one patch spends nothing there and keeps the full couple.
- **A patch that is barely slipping grips** (`CFG.epsStick = 0.020 m/s`): static
  friction bounds it by μs and it holds the puck to that surface — target = the
  surface's motion under the puck's centre — instead of chasing the slip. A grip is
  distributed, so it applies no lever. This is what settles the roller: once the puck
  rolls on the floor patch the slip there is ~0, the floor grips at μs, and the disk's
  μk drive (`μk·a` vs `μs·(1−a)`, `a ≈ 0.47`) cannot move it.

### 2. Second return rule (`cfg.js`, `rules.js`, `render.js`, `index.html`)

`recycleMode: 'advance'` = the wedge behind the disk (base = the whole left wall, apex =
the disk centre) plus the launch lane (x from `CFG.launchMinX` to the right wall, floor
to ceiling). `inReturnZone()` shares one wound-triangle test between the two rules;
`render.js` bakes each rule as `{path (evenodd with the guard hole), line}` and labels
both halves of the new one. Third radio in the panel.

## Notes for whoever comes next

- The sticking test is the slip at the *sampled contact point*. For a fully covered puck
  that point is the puck centre, so a puck spinning in place on the disk keeps the μk
  rate all the way down — while a straddling puck's patches sit off-centre and cross
  into the μs rate below `|om| ≈ 0.5 rad/s`. It is the same rule applied at the contact
  the model samples; making the two agree would mean sampling more than one point.
- The roller's creep along the rim is bounded by the grip, not removed: it still walks
  a few centimetres before it sleeps. A rolling-resistance term would remove that too,
  at the cost of another coefficient.
- `experiments/logs/field.png` is regenerated by `field-preview` and is gitignored.
