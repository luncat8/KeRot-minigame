# 0.1.6 worklog — seats (N players × 4 balls) and red-area scoring

Reference prompt: `0.1.6-draft.md`, plan: `archive/0.1.6-plan.md`. Turns the
single-player pool of 0.1.1–0.1.5 into a seat-based game: every seat has 4 balls and
pushes one per turn, and the score is no longer a sum of field values read at the end
but a *rate* — the red area of the big disk a seat's pucks cover — integrated over the
turn window, for every seat at once.

## 1. Seats (`js/players.js`, new)

A seat is `{ type, balls, score }`; `SEAT_COLORS` lives here and is the one palette
(board HUD, panel, puck rings). `nextSeat(roster, from)` is the turn order: the first
seat with a ball left, starting at `from` and wrapping, −1 when the game is over — so
a seat that ran out is skipped without a special case, and a seat that has a ball but
no room for it on the board is retired the same way.

`js/physics.js` gains `own: Uint8Array` — the seat a puck belongs to, set by
`spawnPuck(w, x, y, owner)` (owner defaults to 0, so the physics experiments keep
their 3-argument calls) and copied by every swap-remove and by `predict.js`.
`MAX_PUCKS` 32 → 40: 8 seats × 4 balls can all be on the board at once.

## 2. Scoring (`js/score.js`, new)

    seat.score += dt · Σ_pucks ∫∫_{puck ∩ disk} R(x, y) dA

R is the red channel of the pixel `field.js` paints there, so the score is literally
"how much red you are sitting on". It is monotone in the field value — 55 at v = −1,
245 at v = 0, 255 at v = +1 — which is worth knowing: the cold lobes are what a player
pays for, and the difference between pale (245) and hot (255) is small on purpose,
because that is what the palette does.

Two things make it cheap enough for every puck every frame (measured, `experiments/
scoring.js`):

- **R from a grid, not from the field.** The disk's red channel is baked once into a
  97² grid over the disk's bounding square (1.7 cm cells) and read with a clamped
  bilinear, so a cell costs two multiplies instead of the field's four `exp()`. The
  grid is in disk-local metres — the disk's rotation is applied to the sample point at
  the puck's centre, never to the grid, and a puck riding the disk has a constant rate,
  which is the whole point.
- **Exact area, sampled mean.** The covered area is not a cell count — that would
  quantise a puck hanging over the rim to the cell size (measured: 5 % of a puck, and
  ~10 % of the lens, at d = 0.806) — but the circle-circle lens `physics.diskOverlap`
  already integrates, one acos per puck. The puck's own equal-area sample pattern (the
  same 64 cells the friction uses, exported from physics.js) then only estimates the
  *mean* red over that area, which a coarse pattern does well.

| check | result |
|---|---|
| integral vs 240² midpoint quadrature, 180 placements × 4 disk angles | worst **0.69 %**, mean bias **−0.009 %** |
| covered area vs the analytic lens, d = 0…R+r | **exact** (8e-9 m² at the branch point d = R−r, acos cancellation) |
| cost, 32 pucks all over the disk | 0.88 µs per puck, **0.028 ms per frame** (physics is 0.07 ms, budget 16.7) |
| frame-rate independence (2 s at h vs at 8h) | 15.952 vs 15.951 |

The prompt's fallback (centre + 6 perimeter samples) was not needed: the pattern is
already paid for and the analytic area removes the only error a coarse pattern had.

`scoreTick(w, roster, dt)` is one pass over the pucks that pays every seat at once —
O(pucks), not O(seats × pucks) — called from `Game.afterSteps` with the simulated
`dtSim`, so the total is frame-rate independent.

## 3. Flow (`js/game.js`)

- `armTurn()` takes a ball from the seat on turn, spawns its puck on the launch spot
  with `own = turn`, and preps the bot if the seat is one. No seat has a ball left →
  `settle`.
- The window that pays opens at the push and closes at `handoffT` (4 s) or when the
  board settles; the spin-down at the end is still live board and pays too. Nothing is
  scored while the next seat aims — the window in the prompt is per push.
- A recycled puck goes back to **its owner's** stock (`giveBack`), which is what
  "return to pool (reuse)" means now that the stock is per seat; with `none` a game is
  exactly seats × 4 throws. `CFG.maxThrows` 48 → 96 caps a board that keeps handing
  pucks back.
- Roster edits are live, no reset: `addPlayer` pushes a seat (and starts a new game if
  the board was spent, since there is nothing left to score there), `removePlayer`
  refunds the puck on the launch spot, drops that seat's pucks from the board, splices
  the roster and re-arms if it was his turn.

## 4. Presentation

- `js/render.js`: pucks are stroked in their owner's colour; a HUD along the top of
  the board draws one cell per seat (colour dot, balls left as dots, displayed score),
  lights the seat on turn and drains its turn window underneath.
- `js/ui.js` + `index.html`: a roster block — per seat a colour dot, a type select
  (human / three bots), balls, score and ×, plus "+ add player" (capped at
  `maxPlayers`, and the last seat cannot be removed) — and a final breakdown with the
  winner.
- Display: raw is `red·m²·s`, an arbitrary unit that doubles when the game does, so
  `displayScore` maps it onto 0…999 against a reference game of that size — every ball
  parked on the reddest pixel for the average life of a ball in the game. Measured
  (40 games, 3 seats, `experiments/compete.js`): random 109 ± 92, keeper 426 ± 167,
  hunter 676 ± 217.

## 5. What changed in the shared API

`CFG.poolSize` → `ballsPerPlayer` / `maxPlayers`; `rules.poolOf` / `roundOver` are
gone (the round ends when no seat has a ball); `recyclePucks(w, mode, onBack)` reports
the owner it took a puck from, and `takePucks` / `dropOwned` / `removePuckAt` are the
removals built on one `moveLast`; `physics.totalScore` / `fieldValueOf` are gone (the
old end-of-round field sum is not the score any more); `Game.setPlayer` →
`setSeatType`, `Game.finalScore` → `Game.winner` + per-seat scores, `Render.drawWorld`
now takes one reusable `view` object instead of four positional arguments.
`predict.dScore` is the red area a stroke adds to its own seat's rate, so the bots
optimise what the scoreboard counts.

## Notes for whoever comes next

- The score density is the *drawn* red channel, so changing `fieldColor`'s palette
  changes the game's balance — score twice (once at the old palette, once at the new)
  before touching it.
- A seat's score only grows during resolve windows. A human who thinks for 30 s costs
  everyone 30 s of scoring, himself included; if that ever feels wrong, scoring during
  `aim` is one line in `afterSteps`, but it makes "thinking time" a scoring resource.
- `scoreRef` depends on the number of seats, so adding a seat mid-game rescales the
  displayed numbers (the raw scores are untouched). It is a display scale; if it ever
  needs to be stable, freeze it at `reset()`.
- Recycled pucks still disappear by swap-remove with no fade (0.1.5's open item), and
  a returned puck reappearing as a ball is the only feedback that it happened.

## Post-0.1.6 browser fix: duplicate `_ov` broke `js/score.js` entirely

Symptom in the page: `Identifier '_ov' has already been declared (at score.js:1:1)`
followed by `displayScore is not defined` in `drawHud`. Both classic scripts share one
global scope, and `js/physics.js` and `js/score.js` each declared a top-level
`const _ov` scratch object for `diskOverlap`. A duplicate `const` is a parse-time
error, so the whole of `score.js` (`displayScore`, `scoreRate`, `scoreTick`,
`puckRate`, `puckArea`, `scoreRef`) never executed — the node experiments were immune
because they `require()` the files as modules.

Fix: renamed score.js's scratch object to `_sov` (js/score.js:65,74). No behaviour
change. Validation: `experiments/client-smoke.js` OK (render, seats, score, panel,
now including the HUD that was the failing path), `experiments/scoring.js` OK
(worst integral error 0.69 %, 454 ns/puck), and a top-level name-collision scan over
all of `js/*.js` (const/let/var/function/class) is clean — worth running once more
before adding another browser-only file.
