## 1. Tuning limits (draft: max rot 4.000 rad/s, speed range 0.8...4)

- `CFG.omegaMax = 4.000`: the ω slider caps at 4.000 rad/s (label `4.000 rad/s`).
  Default stays `autoOmega()` ≈ 0.783 rad/s (the 90 % rule); cranking ω is the
  arcade knob and produces the documented "meat grinder" warning.
- `CFG.vMin = 0.80`, `CFG.vMax = 4.00`: every stroke speed lies in this range. The
  pointer drag maps `0..dragFull` onto `vMin..vMax` (a 1 mm drag already throws
  `vMin` — no sub-minimal throws), arrow-key power nudges clamp to the same range.
  At `vMax` the coasting stopping distance is `v²/(2μ_k g)` ≈ 27 m ≫ board, i.e.
  hard throws live off wall bounces (`e_wall = 0.35`) — that is the requested
  arcade range and the exact TOI solver handles it (fuzzed to 50 m/s).

## 2. Puck recycling: pool, radio group, end condition

### 2.1 Model

The player holds a **pool** of `CFG.poolSize = 8` pucks. A throw moves a puck from
the pool to the board; a puck **recycled** by a return rule goes back to the pool
and can be thrown again. The round ends when the pool is empty (8 pucks parked on
the board) or `CFG.maxThrows = 48` is reached (hard cap so a recycling-only bot can
never loop forever) — then the disk spins down (`settle`) and the score is the ΣV of
the remaining pucks (recycled pucks are gone and do not score). With recycling off
this reduces exactly to the classic "8 throws then score".

Recycling runs at RESOLVE end (all pucks at rest / locked) and again at SETTLE end,
and applies to **sleeping** pucks only — a puck riding the disk is still in play.
Pucks touching the disk (`dist ≤ diskR + puckR`) are never recycled: they can be
dragged back into play by the rim, and the zones must not eat scoring pucks.

### 2.2 Radio group (return rule)

| mode | rule | recyclable area |
|---|---|---|
| `none` | keep all (classic 8-throw game) | 0 % |
| `behind` | behind-disk half: `x < diskX`, disk exempt | ~14.5 % |
| `zones` | painted board gutters (below) | ~28 % |

`zones` is the default. The three form a generosity ladder (classic < behind <
zones) and look different on the ice; `behind` is the draft's blunt positional rule,
`zones` is the designed map.

### 2.3 Zone design (draft: "probably 20 % of map... think good size and placement")

First, where do flung pucks actually die? A death-map experiment (40 random-bot
games, one sample per puck) shows two bands: **the left shelf** (49 % of deaths,
`x < diskX`, concentrated at `x < 0.6`) and **the wall bands** (46 % at
`y < 0.25` / `y > 1.95`, on both sides). A left-shelf-only design (the obvious
"crescent + channel") provably collapses to the `behind` rule — the two radio
options would play ~95 % the same — so it is rejected.

The chosen design is a **U of grated melt-gutters** hugging three boards:

```
lagoon:  x < 0.60                 (the cold left shelf — where the disk throws its dead)
streams: y < 0.22 | y > 1.98      (the ejection lanes along the top/bottom boards)
```

- Sizes come from the death bands (lagoon reaches the shelf shoulder at x≈0.6,
  streams cover most of the wall band). Recyclable area ≈ **28 %** — fatter than the
  draft's 20 % guess because the measured bands are wide; thin gutters leave
  clogging debris at 0.2 m from the walls and play worse than a bold U.
- Measured behaviour: **98 % of dead pucks land inside the zones**; the remainder
  (disk shoulder, launch corner) stays as recoverable debris — the "zones to not
  return": the open ice, the apron next to the disk, and the launch lane keep their
  pucks, because those can still be knocked back into play.
- Look: one prebuilt `Path2D`, translucent teal fill, 45° hatch clipped to the
  zones, dashed outline, small rotated `RETURN` labels. `behind` mode draws the
  half-plane tint + dashed midline + the exempt margin arc around the disk. Zones
  draw under the disk so the field texture stays clean.

Recycle = swap-remove from the typed arrays (`n` stays dense; indices are display
labels only).

## 3. Ghost-trajectory preview

`js/predict.js`: `createPredictor(cfg)` allocates one extra world + a
`Float64Array` path buffer once; `predictStroke(pred, src, i, vx, vy, maxT)` clones
the world (`cloneInto`), throws puck `i`, and steps the clone at the real `h` until
it settles or `maxT` (= `maxResolve`), sampling the thrown puck's centre every 8
steps (60 Hz) plus the exact final point. Results: path, end position, end speed,
`locked`, elapsed, and `dScore = totalScore(after) − totalScore(before)`.

- Rendered as a 6-chunk alpha-faded trail + ghost puck at the predicted resting
  point (dashed ring when the stroke would LOCK). Replaces the old straight
  "preview line"; the checkbox is now "ghost trajectory".
- Recomputed only when the stroke changes, throttled to 12.5 Hz during a drag
  (≈2–4 ms per re-sim at 8 pucks — measured against the bench).
- Determinism of `stepWorld` makes the ghost exact (same code path as the game).

## 4. Player types, bots, competition

### 4.1 Bots (`js/bots.js`, node-safe)

Interface: `chooseStroke(name, w, cfg, i, pred, rng) → botOut {vx, vy}`. All
randomness through `makeRng(seed)` (xorshift32) so runs are reproducible. Stroke
speeds always lie in `[vMin, vMax]`.

| bot | idea | cost |
|---|---|---|
| `random` | flings at the disk centre ±69°, uniform speed — baseline | none |
| `keeper` | physics heuristic, no simulation: brake to a stop on the rotating hot lobe (`v = √(2μ_k g d)·1.05` clamped) with small jitter | none |
| `hunter` | 16 stratified candidate strokes around the disk direction, each simulated to rest with `predictStroke`; keeps the best `dScore + 0.05·locked` | ~16 predictions/throw |

### 4.2 In-browser play

Panel "player" select: `human | bot — random | bot — keeper | bot — hunter`. A bot
aims on entering AIM (its stroke shows as the normal aim arrow + ghost), thinks for
`CFG.botDelay = 0.7 s`, then throws. Human pointer/Space input is ignored while a
bot plays (R/Pause/Reset stay live).

### 4.3 Headless competition (`experiments/compete.js`)

`node experiments/compete.js [games] [seed0]` — every bot plays whole games under
identical rules (`recycleMode`, pool, `maxThrows`) and per-game seeds, in pure node
(no DOM). Records per bot: score mean/std/min/max, ms per game, mean throws,
mean recycled, mean final board count, mean sim time. Results:

- `experiments/results/competition.json` — full detail of the latest run +
  a config snapshot (so future comparisons know the ruleset);
- `experiments/results/history.csv` — one row appended per bot per run, for
  comparing new bots over time (committed; small).

## 7. Open decisions

1. Recycling is automatic on rest — no "declare/cash out" button; live score is
   always shown and Reset banks nothing. Later idea: "end round now".
2. `maxThrows = 48` is a rule (both game and bots), not just a safety.
3. Bot evaluation uses `dScore` of the whole board (includes knock-on effects);
   the 0.05 lock bonus is a tie-breaker toward stable placements.
4. Zone shapes are axis-aligned rects (grated industrial look, trivial hit test) —
   organic blob shapes were considered and dropped for readability.
