# 0.6.1 — paint under the ice, lamps in it, a voice per colour

Scope: the user asked for field markings that read as painted *under* ice, a different
hit tone per puck colour, "some blurry mirror of ceiling lamps in the ice in some
places", and a free hand on further improvements. The prompt is kept in `0.6.1-draft.md`,
the forkable architecture in `0.6.1-plan.md`.

## Architecture

The round stays inside the 0.6 opt-in: one page, no new file, no dependency, nothing
downloaded for a plain board.

- `js/render.js` gained a sixth optional hook, `skin.surface(ctx, view, cfg)`, called
  once per frame after the disk, its rim and the eject ring and before the black holes,
  the launch box, the ghost, the aim and the stones — inside the board clip. It is the
  top of the ice: over every marking, under everything that is not paint. The base draw
  path is unchanged apart from that one guarded call.
- `js/fx-glass.js` (19,177 → 29,388 bytes) grew four bakes, all fixed-size and drawn
  through `drawImage`: `glazeTex` 512² (ice thickness over the disk), `milkTex` 432×264
  (the same veil over the whole board), `lampTex[0..1]` 432×264 (ceiling reflections in
  board space) and `softTex` 192² (scattered paint, baked once per disk schema).
- `games/kerot.js` was rebuilt because it carries the renderer (177,563 → 177,843
  bytes); it still contains no FX code. `js/fx.js` is untouched.

## Under-ice markings

- `softTex` is the shipped field painter (`bakeFieldCanvas`) run at 60 px/m, drawn over
  the sharp paint at 0.76 (classic) / 0.82 (kerot) alpha inside the rotating frame. It
  is the same field, so it can only soften an edge — the score map, the bot aims and
  the schema shapes cannot move. This replaces the rejected option of blurring the score
  texture (a per-frame repaint that would also move the paint).
- `glazeTex` sits on top: cool milk that is thinner at the skated centre and thicker at
  the cold rim, 30 frost patches, 150 trapped-air bubbles (dark rim, offset bright
  core). The existing brushed polish stays above that, because scratches belong to the
  surface. `milkTex` gives the zone hatch, the launch box and the disk the same thin
  veil, so the zone paint stops being vector-sharp.
- Tuned against the plain render of the same schema: mean RGB inside the disk rose from
  (199,173,201) to (207,185,209) in kerot, from (200,176,204) to (210,192,214) in
  classic — ~10 levels of contrast for a visible two-material look. The pale ring that
  the blur widens around a `rings` schema is the field's own 0 → 1 transition, present
  in the plain render too.
- First pass was too milky (glaze 0.80, board veil 0.80): the red field fell back to
  pale pink and the teal zone to grey. Reduced to 0.58/54 (glaze) and 0.38/0.30 (veil)
  with a weaker central milk stop; the crops then read as depth rather than fog.

## Lamp reflections

- Five lamps in two groups, positioned in board metres clear of the HUD, the zone
  labels and the launch lane. Each bake is an upright elliptical halo, a soft vertical
  smear along the line of sight and the tube as a bright bar with two bloom passes,
  faded at both ends so no cap is drawn in.
- Drawn with globalAlpha ≈ 0.54/0.44 and `globalCompositeOperation = 'lighter'`: normal
  alpha had turned the warm bars grey over the teal zone fill. The two groups breathe
  on separate slow phases (±10 %, 0.37 and 0.48 Hz) and hold mid brightness under
  `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- Reflections live in board space, so they never rotate with the disk — they are the
  room, not the plate. First placement put the upper bars under the scoreboard and the
  glow bled through its translucent cells; both group-0 lamps moved down 0.12 m.

## A voice per colour

- `VOICES[8]` follows `SEAT_COLORS` order: a pentatonic ladder (1.00, 1.10, 1.20, 1.335,
  1.42, 1.60, 1.75, 1.90), a partial spread per seat and a decay that shortens as the
  note rises. `glassPing` takes pitch/spread/decay instead of two constants; the rail
  tap uses the stone's own voice, a two-colour contact rings at the geometric mean of
  the pair, and `stoneDetune(uid)` adds ±1.2 % so same-colour stones are not a unison.
  Still three oscillators per contact, still rate-limited, still derived from velocity
  jumps in `frame()`.
- `nearestTouch` replaced `nearPuck` (index instead of a boolean) so the second voice
  of a contact can be read without a second scan.

## Validation

- `node experiments/fx-smoke.js` — **OK**. New gates: `surface` draws the veil plus two
  lamp groups, the disk stacks scatter + glaze + polish, the scattered paint is baked
  once and rebakes only on a schema change, a rail tap of seat 3 rings 1.335× seat 0's
  tap, and a red/blue contact rings at √1.9 of the same-colour contact. The first run
  caught the pair test reporting no oscillators (the pucks were still asleep) and a
  wrong pitch comparison that ignored the bright/dull base.
- `node experiments/client-smoke.js` — **OK**; six hooks reachable, `surface` once per
  frame, and detaching the skin still restores the base path.
- `node tools/build-minigame.js && --check`; `node experiments/minigame-smoke.js` — OK
  (bundle, isolation, live match, 197 frames).
- `determinism`, `settings-revive`, `scoring`, `spin`, `time-warp`, `speed-vote`,
  `zone-catch` — all gates passed. `vote-rules` was started and left running past the
  session budget; this round touches no physics, vote or scoring file, and its result
  files were not kept.
- `node --check` over every changed script; `git diff --check` clean.
- `node experiments/render-frames.js --glass experiments/logs/after` and
  `node experiments/render-frames.js experiments/logs/after-plain` produced 12 real
  1798×1100 PNGs (glass and plain, classic/kerot × classic/circles/rim6) used for the
  tuning above. They stay in the ignored `experiments/logs/`.
- Software-canvas timing at 1798×1100: plain 0.75 ms/frame, glass 1.30 ms/frame — the
  three added draws per frame cost ~0.2 ms, a sprite bake ~2 ms once.

## Next step

The sound layer still has no volume control, which is now the most obvious gap: eight
pitches at one loudness is exactly where a player reaches for a slider. A `volume`
range in the panel, a `vol=` link key, a master gain scale and one more preset flag is
about thirty lines. After that, panning each contact by the stone's board x-position
(`createStereoPanner`, guarded like every other optional node) is the natural next step
for a board you hear; both need the same real-browser check as the audio already does.
