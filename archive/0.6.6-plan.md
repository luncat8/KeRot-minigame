# 0.6.6 plan — cut-circle return zones: 3-rounded and 6-hemi

Two more return-zone shapes in the same rule group as `hatch`, `wavy`, `rounded`
and `arcs`. The shapes are rules, not skins: whatever is drawn is exactly the
region the rule tests, as the plain wedge already is.

## Settings

| key | CFG / page | values | URL |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| return zone shape | `CFG.zoneStyle` | `hatch` (shipped), `wavy`, `rounded`, `arcs`, `3-rounded`, `6-hemi` | `zones=` |

The key is live (no reset): the renderer rebakes its zone paths when it sees a
new value, and the ghost is re-simulated. The `default` preset keeps the shipped
shape. The Snowfall minigame keeps the shipped shapes (it plays without return
zones).

## Return zones

- **hatch** — the shipped wedge (or fan) minus the guard, 45° stripes, dashed outline.
- **wavy** — the same region and outline; each stripe wavers on two incommensurate sines,
  phases stepped by the golden angle.
- **rounded** and **arcs** are *soft* zones: the region `zoneField > 0` of one clearance
  field in `js/rules.js`, built with a polynomial smooth minimum (k = 0.3 m):
  - *rounded*, wedge side: `smin(guard, hole reach − puckR …, side(hole → disk centre) …)`.
    The hole terms make the outline hug each void; k is more than twice the 8 cm
    hole ↔ guard gap, so the outline bridges it with a fillet, burying the side lines,
    which only close the zone. No straight edge remains. Fan side: the fan's sides with
    the guard corners rounded. The lines are the field's levels every 8 cm — inset
    copies of the outline, rounded where it is.
  - *arcs*: `smin(guard, vertical through the disk centre)` — one curve from wall to wall
    around the disk; the holes lie inside the zone. Lines: circles around the disk
    centre every 8 cm, clipped to the zone.
  - The launch lane joins either (`max` with the strip's field) in `advance`.
- **3-rounded** and **6-hemi** are *cut* zones: the same `zoneField > 0` region, but
  built with a plain minimum, so the outline is the circles and straight sides
  themselves, cut sharp where they meet. Both keep a strip of ice between rim and
  outline: every cut circle is grown by `puckR + ZONE_CUT_GAP` (gap = 0.08 m, one
  inset-line step), so a puck can sit in the gap without being recycled.
  - *3-rounded*: the wedge (or fan) cut by three bigger circles — the disk and each
    hole. The outline is their arcs and the wedge's straight sides. The lines are
    the field's levels every 8 cm — inset copies of the outline, sharp where it is.
  - *6-hemi*: the half-board through the disk centre cut by one bigger circle
    around the disk — one circular outline, the holes inside the zone, as in
    `arcs` but cut sharp and with the gap. Lines: the field's levels every 8 cm.
  - The launch lane joins either (`max` with the strip's field) in `advance`.
- Rendering: marching squares over the same `zoneField`, chained into polylines so
  dashes run on along a contour. Outline: 1 cm grid, the field clamped just outside
  the walls so every contour closes into a loop (fill evenodd, the wall pieces fall
  outside the board clip). Inset lines: 2.5 cm grid. A rebake (style change or board
  geometry change in the fit view) costs ≈ 25 ms for both modes.
- Holes inside a zone need no extra rule: `absorbBlackHoles` removes a puck in the step
  it touches a void, long before a handoff could recycle it.

## Cost

A zone test is one `zoneField` call. The soft pair pays a smooth minimum per term
(≈ 120 ns per call on the wedge side); the cut pair pays a plain minimum of circle
distances (≈ 27 ns). A bot decision makes a few thousand zone tests (two per
simulated candidate per puck on the board), so the difference is under a
millisecond against tens of milliseconds of physics simulation per decision —
measured bot-decision time is the same for every zone shape. The bake is the only
place the field is paid at scale (≈ 165 000 calls, ≈ 25 ms), and it is rare.

## Validation

`node experiments/zone-shapes.js`: drawn field region = rule region on 40 000 random
points per shape and mode; 3-rounded keeps the gap clear of the disk and the holes
and stays inside the wedge; 6-hemi keeps the gap clear of the disk and holds the
holes; the lane stays in; all 72 shape × rule combinations draw; URL keys and the
default preset. Renders: `node experiments/render-frames.js --mode=advance
--zone=3-rounded` and `--zone=6-hemi` (and `--mode=zones` for the fan side).
