# 0.5.6 worklog — fit view, transport on top, pointer aiming

Draft: `0.5.6-draft.md` (the three prompts of this round and the reading of them).

## Review of the previous pass

`archive/0.5.5-worklog.md` fixed the horizontal-phone clipping and the top gap, and left
one next step: make the transport bar reachable without scrolling the panel. Reading it
against the three new prompts, its fix was right but partial in two ways:

1. **The board still loses width to the panel on every screen.** `#panel` is a 320 px
   flex item (clamped to 188–280 px on small screens), so a 1280 px desktop board is
   fitted to 920 px and a 740 px landscape phone to ~450 px. The prompt's "game field
   just fit screen" asks for the opposite default.
2. **A fit box can still carry a letterbox band.** With `align-items: flex-start` the
   top gap is gone *when the board's height binds*, but a box wider than 3.6 : 2.2 fits
   the canvas by its height and leaves the box's surplus as a band — and if the canvas
   were ever centred (the new drawer changes the box width on every open/close), the band
   appears **above** the board, which is exactly the complaint the previous round fixed.
   Fitting by height alone and pinning to the top removes the band by construction.

The rest of the pass holds: `#app` pinned to the visible viewport, the safe-area
padding, `main.js`'s `visualViewport` sync and the `render.js` CSS-size early-return
are all kept and now also carry the fit view (the fit box is anchored to `#app` so the
mobile-URL-bar fix applies to it too).

  the CSS keys both shapes off; the module also owns the ☰ button, the drawer's
  open/close (a pointer leaving it, a tap out on the board, `Esc`), and calls the board's
  refit whenever the panel changes the box width.
- `index.html`: the fit view gives the board box `absolute` placement inside `#app` (top,

  The side layout's old rules are untouched.
- `js/render.js` `resize()`: in the fit view a box wider than the board's aspect binds by
  height alone (`wpx = hpx * aspect`), and `oy = 0` — the canvas is flush with the box's
  top edge in every shape.
- `js/ui.js` / `js/presets.js` / `js/main.js`: the panel's `view` select, the `?view=`
  URL key (`fit` is the shipped value and never written into a link), `View.bind(refit)`
  at boot and `UI.applySetup` honouring `setup.view`.
- `minigame/entry.js` is not touched by the views: the story slot keeps its own compact
  layout and its own chrome (the ☰ button lives in the standalone panel only).

## Transport on top

`#actions` is the first child of `#panel`: Throw, Clear, auto-ω, Reset, Pause and ☰ in
that order, `position: sticky` so it stays while the settings scroll. In the side layout
it is a wrapping row at the top of the panel (it used to sit mid-panel under the roster);
in the fit view it is the rail's column, and the drawer pins it back into a row so the
settings have the full width below it. `UI.actions()` disables Throw/Clear while no
human stroke is possible and mirrors the pause label; Reset and auto-ω stay live.

## Input

- `js/cfg.js`: `aimGrabMargin: 0.22` (grab ring = `puckR + margin` = 0.33 m, exported as
  `aimGrab(cfg)`), `doubleTapT: 350` ms, `doubleTapSlop: 0.24` m.
- `js/game.js` `bindInput`:
  - **Aim**: a pointer outside the puck aims — the stroke's length is the pointer's
    distance past the grab ring, `vMax` at `dragFull` beyond it, so the board point under
    the finger is where the stroke ends; the angle is the pointer's bearing. Every pointer
    event re-anchors the speed at `vMin` and the angle at that bearing, so the stroke is
    absolute (a press places the arrow; a drag sweeps it) and nothing an earlier event
    set can scale a later one. Inside the ring the power holds at `vMin` — dragging back
    home is a soft throw, not a lost stroke.
  - **Throw**: two taps on the puck (`puckR`) inside `doubleTapT` and `doubleTapSlop`
    queue the throw — it is committed between fixed steps, like Space. The *second* tap
    may land anywhere within the slop (a finger drifts while double-tapping; the gesture
    still means the puck), while the first must hit the puck. A mouse or pen click on
    the puck throws in one click. A tap press never moves the stroke, so the finger's
    lift-slide cannot re-aim the throw it is making.
  - **Place (mouse/pen)**: a press on the board while nothing is aimed places the arrow
    and is not dragged by the click's own release.
  - `pointercancel` still clears a stroke that left the board, and leaves a placed one.
- `js/render.js` `drawAim`: the arrow starts at the grab ring (its length is the power),
  and a faint dashed circle shows the ring while the puck is armed.
- `minigame/entry.js`: the adapter's own pointer handlers take the same shape (press
  outside the ring aims with `dragFull` clamped, press on the puck is the throw — one
  click for a mouse, two taps for touch). Its duplicated `aimFrom`/`pointerDown` are
  ~15 lines and keep their own `draw()`/`updateControls()` cadence, because the adapter
  drives its own loop.
- **Behaviour kept**: shift precision gains, arrow nudges, `Escape`/`Space`/`R`, the drag
  mapping (`0.9 m → vMax`) and the ghost preview. The stroke the tests see is unchanged:
  half drag = mid range, shift = 1/32 speed, 1/8 aim.

### Pointer follow-up

Review found that pointer samples reset the Shift anchor on every move, the first mouse
press could never become a drag, and the rendered arrow tip extended past the mapped point.
The standalone page and Snowfall slot now share one pointer handler. Drag from the puck or
press elsewhere to aim, including the release position; tap the puck once to throw on release.
Shift applies to movement deltas and releasing it does not discard fine adjustments.

Validated with client and minigame smoke tests, determinism, settings-revive, and a rebuilt
bundle. Next: try the gesture on a phone and tune the six-pixel tap tolerance if needed.

## Fit pane correction

The previous renderer contained the board at its native aspect ratio in both modes,
so pinning the canvas at the top left still left an unused strip. Fit now sizes the
canvas and maps the complete world to both dimensions of the pane. The panel takes
its actual width in the flex row; its border marks the split even when settings open.
Side view retains aspect containment. Client smoke passed, including a 900 × 300
pane and its bottom-right pointer mapping. Next check: try aiming on an ultrawide
screen to assess the visual stretch.

## Proportional pieces and swipe navigation

The earlier fit filled the canvas by stretching a fixed playing field. Fit now
changes the dimensions of the ice instead: the disk stays circular, the launch
lane follows the right wall, and rendering, collisions, return rules and bots
use the same geometry. Resizing preserves the current match.

The collapse button and rightward menu swipe leave a small reopen tab. An idle
board accepts horizontal pointer drags for help (right) or menu (left); the same
eligibility check reserves human aiming gestures for the game. Vertical scrolling
and slider gestures in the panel are not drawer commands.

Validation: client-smoke and minigame-smoke passed after rebuilding the bundle.
