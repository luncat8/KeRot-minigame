5. **Bot votes, three iterations.** Each was caught by measurement:
   - *"Best red reachable" as the capture term.* It is flat on classic (the centre is
     red at any speed), so speeding up looked free. Replaced by `holdableRate`: the
     red averaged over what the disk holds.
   - *Denial summed over every enemy.* For room this is a symmetric effect, and the
     sum made the hunter and black hole vote up every round. One `client-smoke` game
     reached ω 1.45 rad/s, 48 throws and 35 votes. Now an enemy throw counts
     denial / (N − 1); riders still count in full. The same game: ω 0.783 → 0.846,
     18 throws, 4 votes.
   - *Noise per candidate on the plateau.* It fragmented equal plateaus, and the
     hunter picked at random between "speed up" and "brake hard" — both shed the
     enemy rider, the second by tangential deceleration. Now the plateau is found
     on noiseless values, and the pick prefers the nearest of equal speeds.
6. **Gates and measurement.**

What the tables say:
- **`turn` hands every throw to the other N − 1 seats.** They want that thrower to
  have less room, so the speed climbs: mean 0.90 – 1.05 with change votes, 1.21 – 1.38
  with exact ones. Scores drop by a third to two thirds, and games run 16 – 117 s longer.
  On the default bots with exact votes it became a 65-throw slog: at 1.4 rad/s
  nothing holds, so everything is returned.
- **Exact votes let one seat move the average by up to omegaMax / N.** Tallies move
  ω by 0.34 – 0.67 instead of 0.07 – 0.16, and shed 3.2 – 6.8 riders per game.
- **`round` + `delta` stays on the configured speed** (mean 0.72 – 0.81). It costs
  4 – 7 s per game (about 4 votes), keeps the throw count, and sheds 0.9 – 2.0
  riders per game. It is the only vote rule that leaves the game recognisable.
- **The default is `round` + `delta`.** The prompt's ticks were "before each player
  turn" + "increase / decrease", with the explicit "I don't know what is better". The
  change mode is the same; the timing is the round.

## Decisions taken without asking

1. **The default rule is `round` + `delta`,** on the measurement above. `turn` and
   `exact` stay selectable.
2. **Bots vote.** They cast their votes as the vote opens. Otherwise a bot table
   would never exercise the rule, and nothing about it could be measured.
3. **A vote is for one tally.** After counting, every vote goes back to don't care,
   the stated default.
4. **No vote before a game's first turn.** "1 second after the turn timer" has no
   timer to follow there. A new game starts from the configured speed, which is
   already what everyone saw.
5. **The vote phase scores for nobody.** A turn's window pays the seat whose throw
   made it, and the vote is nobody's turn.
6. **A tally ramps the disk over 1 s; it does not jump.** A rider pays for the
   tangential acceleration, and so does the spin-down at the end. The physics change
   is exact for a steady disk (bit-identical gates).
7. **A change vote is bounded to ±0.5 rad/s.** That is the old default speed's
   reach to a still disk and to about 1.3 rad/s. Exact votes span 0 – omegaMax.
8. **The configured speed belongs to the settings, a voted one to the game.** The
   next game starts from the configured speed, and a shared link carries it. A
   direct speed change (slider, auto-ω, preset, URL) is a new configured speed and
   jumps the disk, the old admin behaviour, under any rule.
9. **The `solo` preset turns the rule off:** a vote of one is the slider. The
   `default` preset names the rule, so it stays the way back from anywhere.
10. **Rule changes.** Changing the rule or the mode drops the votes held, since a
    value means something else in the other mode. Switching the rule off closes a
    running vote uncounted.

## Next step

**Make voting quick for a human.**
- Votes can be cast at any time, but only from the panel. That is far from the
  board on a phone and off-screen on a small window.
- Suggestion:
  - two keys, `[` and `]`, that step the human's vote by 0.05 (the turn's seat when
    several humans share the keyboard);
  - `=` for keep, and `\` for don't care;
  - a one-line board hint while a vote is open.
- Then measure how often a human's vote, cast during the turns, survives to the
  tally against the bots' averaging.
- After that, the multiplayer plan (`0.1.2-plan.md`) can carry the vote as it is.
  A seat's vote is three fields, and the tally is one function on the host.
