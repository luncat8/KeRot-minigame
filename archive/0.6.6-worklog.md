# 0.6.6 worklog — cut-circle return zones: 3-rounded and 6-hemi

## Review of the previous round

0.6.5 added the soft zones (`rounded`, `arcs`) as one smooth-min clearance field
traced by marching squares, so drawn = rule. Its open items stay open: the rounded
fan's fillet may want a smaller k than the wedge side, and bot balance on `arcs`
(whose fan covers the whole right half) is unmeasured. Nothing in this round
depends on them.

## Reading the request

The user imagines `rounded` and `arcs` as cut circles but accepts the smooth
shape, and asks whether the smooth shape is heavy for the bot. Then two new
shapes: **3-rounded** — cut from the zone a bigger outline of the holes and the
big disk, with a reasonable gap; **6-hemi** — the zone outline is a circular
surface (see `archive/temp/6-hemis.webp`).

## Decisions

- **Geometry.** 3-rounded is the wedge/fan cut by three bigger circles (disk +
  both holes); 6-hemi is the half-board through the disk centre cut by one bigger
  circle (holes inside, as in `arcs`). Both are the cut-circle version of their
  smooth counterpart, with the gap. The wedge sides stay straight in 3-rounded;
  the half-board's vertical stays straight in 6-hemi — the circular part is the
  cut circle, which is what "circular surface" asks for.
- **Gap 0.08 m.** One inset-line step: the outline sits 18 cm from the disk rim
  (puckR + gap), so a puck can sit in the strip without being recycled. Zone area
  stays close to the shipped wedge (advance 1.66 vs 1.79 m², fan 2.35 vs 2.41 m²),
  so the game balance barely moves.
- **Plain `min`, same field, same renderer.** No new drawing code: the field's
  zero level is the Boolean cut region, and marching squares traces it exactly as
  it traces the soft pair. Sharp corners come out within a grid cell, which the
  drawn = rule check allows. An analytic arc drawing would be crisper but would
  need circle–triangle clipping and a second drawn = rule argument.
- **Cost answer: no, not significant.** Per call, the soft wedge test is ≈ 120 ns
  and the cut test ≈ 27 ns (500 000 calls, warmed). A bot decision makes a few
  thousand zone tests (two `recoverableCount` passes per simulated candidate per
  puck), so the difference is ≈ 0.3 ms against ≈ 235 ms of physics simulation per
  full decision — measured decision time is 240 / 233 / 238 ms for hatch /
  rounded / arcs, the same within noise. The bake pays the field ≈ 165 000 times
  (≈ 25 ms for both modes) but only on a style change or geometry change. The
  cut pair is still 3–4× cheaper per test and its bake is a little cheaper
  (26 / 23 ms vs 41 ms for rounded), but neither matters for bot speed.
