## Review of the previous task (0.1.1) — issues found and fixed

1. `game.js` `nudge()` used `shiftKey` for the angle only; the drag had one gain.
   Both are now precision-aware (`CFG.preciseGain`).
2. Pucks had no material rotation at all, so "rotational impulse from friction against
   both the ordinary floor and the big_disk" (0.1.0 brief) existed only as the locked
   pucks' orbit. Added `th`/`om`, drawn as 6 sectors.
3. Disk friction sampled the surface velocity at the puck **centre**; a puck
   straddling the rim is now dragged by the surface under the patch it actually
   touches (the patch centroid).
4. The ghost preview only re-simulated on aim changes — a slider move left a stale
   ghost. `Game.invalidateGhost()` is now called from every physics input.
5. `experiments/energy.js` printed a FAIL without setting its flag (silent gate).
6. **Zombie rim pucks (pre-existing).** A puck that came to rest jammed on the rim
   (`a ≈ 0.4`, `v = 0`) stayed `SLIDE` forever — the floor patch cancels the disk patch
   every step, while the sleep gate demanded the surface-matched relative velocity.
   Every later resolve then burned the full `maxResolve = 8 s`. Found by the new
   browser-layer smoke (the bot round never reached `SCORED`); confirmed identical in
   the 0.1.1 build (`git show 2bcd271:js/physics.js`, same frozen state after 5 s), so
   it is a pre-existing artifact of the two-surface model, not a spin regression.
   Fixed by accepting the jam in the sleep gate (§2.4 of the plan).

## Design corrections made during implementation (folded into the plan)

1. **The spin gate in the lock condition collapses the lock band.** Requiring
   `|ω_d − om| ≤ epsSpin` before locking delayed capture to ~0.19 s, during which the
   puck drifts out of the hold radius much faster than the spin ramps (kinetic friction
   cannot supply ω²d). Measured: the co-rotating 0.8 R case slid out to 1.255 R and
   slept instead of locking (`lock-band` FAIL). Locking now snaps the spin exactly as it
   already snapped the velocity — the ramp only governs the *pre-capture* behaviour.
2. **The floor patch wins the rim jam.** Both drags use μk, so the balance is `a ≤ 1−a`;
   that clause is what lets a jammed puck sleep.
3. **Puck-puck friction is Coulomb-capped by the same event's normal impulse**, so a
   separating/overlapping contact gets no friction and the head-on case stays exactly
   as it was (mass cancellation untouched).
4. **Wall rub keeps `wallTanKeep` verbatim**, applied to the contact-point tangential
   speed: identical to 0.1.1 for `ω = 0`, and the impulse's moment arm spins the puck.
5. The fan's *drawn* exemption is the guard itself (`diskR + puckR`), not the disk
   circle — the picture and the rule match.
