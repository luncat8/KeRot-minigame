# 0.5.6 draft — fit view, the transport bar on top, and a pointer that aims from outside the puck

User prompts for this round:

```
1  add view mode (default) in what game field just fit screen - right panel (in case of
   horizontal layout)

2  then move transport function to top of panel
   Throw ␣ Clear stroke auto-ω (90%) Reset board Pause

3  improve input
   Throw by double click on circle. aim by drag (or click) that end OUTSIDE of circle
   itself + some radius margin
```

## Reading of the prompts

- **"Game field just fit screen — right panel" is a layout, not a bigger canvas.** The
  standalone page has one shape today: a row with a fixed panel, and the panel costs the
  board up to 320 px of its width on every screen. That is the wrong trade for the
  primary view: the thing being played is the board, and the settings are touched
  between games, not during one. The default view therefore *fits*: the board box takes
  the whole viewport (less a narrow rail), the settings live in a drawer, and the side
  layout is still there as the other option.
- **"Fit" must never leave margin above the canvas.** The old pass removed a centring
  gap, but a *letterbox* is a second source of the same complaint: the board keeps its
  3.6 : 2.2 aspect, so any box that is wider than the board finds the fitted canvas
  shorter than the box, and a centred board then carries a band of empty box above it.
  Fitting the board by the box's *height alone* when that is the binding axis, and
  pinning it to the box's top edge, removes the band by construction — the board is
  flush with the top in every shape the screen takes.
- **The transport bar is where the hand is, not where the settings are.** The five
  buttons a turn needs — throw, clear, auto-ω, reset, pause — sat mid-panel, below the
  roster and the rules, which on a phone is a scroll away. They move to the very top of
  the panel, and in the fit view the panel's top *is* the rail: five cells down the right
  edge (a bar under the board in portrait), reachable with the thumb that already holds
  the phone.
- **"Double click on circle" is a throw gesture, and "outside the circle + margin" is
  where the aim starts.** A pointer on the puck's centre carries no direction, so the
  old rule — drag from the puck — made the first millimetres meaningless and put the
  aiming finger on top of the thing it was aiming. The new rule splits the two: the puck
  (and its double tap) is the trigger, the clear board around it is the aim. The arrow
  runs from the puck to the pointer and its length is the power, so the point the finger
  stands on is the point the stroke ends at — a press is a place, and a drag is a sweep
  of that place.

## Decisions

- **Two view modes, `fit` (the default) and `side`, a URL key.** `body[data-fit]` is the
  switch the CSS keys both shapes off; `js/view.js` owns it, the drawer's tab, its
  open/close and the refit the board needs whenever its box changes. `?view=side` is
  what a link writes when the layout is not the shipped one — `fit` is never in a link,
  because it is what a bare page opens.
- **The panel is the drawing board's rail in the fit view.** Five buttons and ☰, 66 px
  wide, on the right edge; the drawer is the same element widened over the board (not a
  second copy of the panel), so the buttons it shows are one set of nodes and the state
  they read is one set of listeners. In portrait the rail lies under the board and the
  drawer rises from the bottom, where a thumb is.
- **The fit board box is anchored to `#app`, not the viewport.** `#app` is the box
  `js/main.js` keeps pinned to the *visible* viewport (the fix the previous pass made for
  mobile URL bars): `position: fixed; top/height` are written from `visualViewport`. The
  fit box and the drawer hang off it absolutely, so a phone whose URL bar hides or shows
  mid-game cannot leave the board's bottom clipped again.
- **The stroke is absolute on every pointer event.** `aimFrom` re-anchors the speed at
  `vMin` and the angle at the pointer's own bearing on every press and move, so nothing
  an earlier drag left behind can scale a later one, and a press on the board is a
  "place the arrow's end here" rather than a delta from wherever the last drag stopped.
  The grab ring (`puckR + aimGrabMargin`, 0.33 m) is the zero of the power axis: inside
  it the stroke holds at `vMin`, past it `dragFull` (0.9 m) takes it to `vMax`.
- **The throw is the puck's gesture, and it needs a stroke in hand.** Two taps on the
  puck within `doubleTapT` (350 ms) and `doubleTapSlop` (0.24 m) throw the stroke in
  hand; a mouse or pen click on the puck does the same in one click. The tap is consumed
  by the gesture — it does not aim, so a finger that slides a little while it lifts
  cannot re-aim the throw it is making. Space and the Throw button are unchanged.
- **A press on the ring's margin aims, not throws.** The throw gesture reads the puck
  only (`puckR`), so a throw that misses the puck by a little re-aims instead of being
  eaten — and the same hit test is the one `minigame/entry.js` now uses, so the story
  slot and the page aim alike.
- **The old margins are untouched.** The shift precision gains, the arrow nudges, the
  drag mapping and the ghost preview all keep their behaviour; only *where a stroke may
  begin* and *what a click on the puck means* changed.

## Not in this round

- **The rail does not show a label for what is on turn.** The board's HUD already marks
  the seat, and a sixth status cell would eat the rail's width on a 360 px-tall phone.
- **The drawer does not trap focus.** It is a pointer-first surface; keyboard users have
  the whole panel in the side layout (`?view=side`), and `Esc` closes the drawer.
- **The movement of the drag anchor between pointer events is not smoothed.** The stroke
  reads what the pointer says on each event, exactly; a stylus that jitters reads as a
  jittery length, which is the honest mapping of the prompt's "drag that ends outside".
