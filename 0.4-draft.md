## Reading of the prompt

- **kerot-s is a reference, not a merge.** Read it whole, report what is worth taking and
  what is broken, and port the useful ideas in this codebase's own architecture. Its bugs
  are reported, not reproduced (`archive/0.4-worklog.md` has both lists).
- **The mass slider has to do something.** kerot-s has one, and it is a live no-op: under
  load-independent Coulomb friction a lone puck's mass cancels from both its
  deceleration (μ·m·g/m) and the hold limit (ω²d ≤ μs·g); only collisions between
  *unequal* masses feel it, and a slider that sets every mass equal cancels that too.
  Mass needs a door into the physics, and the physical one is the load dependence of ice
  friction: with a Hertzian real contact area A ∝ N^(2/3) and a constant shear strength,
  μ ∝ m^(−1/3). So the slider sets every puck's mass, the μ sliders are the coefficients
  of a 4 kg reference puck, and a heavy puck slides farther (stop distance ∝ m^(1/3)) and
  is flung sooner (hold radius ∝ m^(−1/3)). At 4 kg nothing changes, bit for bit.
- **Disk colour schema = score map.** The score is the red channel of the paint, so a
  colour schema is a rule as much as a look: three schemas — **soft lobes** (the
  current one, default), **3 red circles** (kerot-s's field: three red zones of
  different radii on blue), **6 rim hemispheres** (six red half-circles centred on the
  rim, blue gaps between them). One palette for all of them, with a red channel that
  rises with the field value — kerot-s's palette has red at mid (234) above red at max
  (204), which under red-channel scoring would pay pale more than red.
- **UI colour schema = a look, never the paint.** "classic" (today's teal/blue) and
  "kerot-s": its navy panel and monospace type, its floor (gradient + faint grid) and its
  puck — cream body, drop shadow, two shaded rim quarters that show the puck turning, and
  a centre gauge with a tag for the share of the best possible rate that puck earns. The
  owner's seat colour becomes the puck's ring (kerot-s had one player). The scheme never
  touches the disk paint, the seat colours, the zones or the black holes: those are rules.
- Both selections apply live, mid-game, without a reset: a schema change is a new score
  map from that moment on (a bot that is aiming re-aims), a scheme change is paint only.
